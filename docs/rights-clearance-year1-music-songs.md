# Rights clearance — traditional songs & rhymes for a Year 1 (age 5–6) music textbook

**Purpose:** assess whether **full lyrics** may be printed in a **commercial UK/British-English textbook published 2026** (international Cambridge school, Portugal).
**Checked:** August 2026. Not legal advice — a publisher's rights desk should sign off on anything not marked PD-SAFE.

## Legal test applied

- **UK (CDPA 1988):** literary/musical works — copyright expires **70 years from the end of the year the author died**. Works of **unknown authorship** — **70 years from the end of the year the work was made**, or, if made available to the public within that window, **70 years from that first availability**. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term
- **USA (matters for international distribution):** works published **before 1930** are in the public domain; 1930–1977 publications can run **95 years from publication**.
- A song can have **three separate rights**: (a) the words, (b) the tune, (c) a particular **arrangement / edited version**. A PD tune does not make a modern lyric set free, and vice versa.

## Practical notes for the editor

1. **Tune PD ≠ lyric PD.** Wheels on the Bus is the clearest case: the *Mulberry Bush* tune is free, Verna Hills's words are not until 2061. Same logic for Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes (PD tune, PD anon. words — fine) and This Old Man (PD tune; the 1958 Malcolm Arnold film version is not).
2. **Beware "traditional" recordings.** CoComelon, Super Simple Songs, Raffi, The Wiggles and Little Baby Bum versions carry their own copyrights in the specific wording and arrangement. Set lyrics from a pre-1929 printed source wherever one exists, and credit "Traditional".
3. **US 95-year trap.** Anything first published in the US 1930–1977 can be protected there until 95 years after publication even where the UK term has lapsed — relevant to Down by the Station (1931) and fatal for Wheels on the Bus (1937).
4. **Three easy substitutions** if you want a clean, all-PD book: replace *Wheels on the Bus* with *Here We Go Round the Mulberry Bush* (same tune family, PD), replace *If You're Happy and You Know It* with *Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes* or *Clap Your Hands* traditional wording, and replace *The Runaway Train* with *I've Been Working on the Railroad* or *The Train Is A-Coming*.
5. **Riskier-but-cheap fixes.** For *Miss Polly*, *Five Little Ducks* and *A Sailor Went to Sea*, you can keep the activity and write fresh words to the same singing shape — this removes the clearance question entirely.
