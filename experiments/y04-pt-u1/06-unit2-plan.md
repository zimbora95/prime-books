# Unidade 2 · Histórias da Pequena Biblioteca: teaching plan and page plan

**Scheme of work (source row).** "Unidade 2 - Texto Narrativo - Autores para a infância e tradição popular". It has seven sub-units: 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6, 2.8 and 2.9. Rows 2.4 and 2.7 are headers only.
**Readers.** Native speakers aged 8–9 (Year 4). Curriculum reference: AE 3.º ano, stretching into 4.º ano where the scheme asks for it.
**Language.** PT-PT with AO90 spelling. Use "estás a ler", never the gerund.

## Big idea
Unit 1 ended with the old red postbox becoming the **Pequena Biblioteca da Rua**. In Unit 2, each week a new book appears on one of its seven shelves (estantes) with a label saying «Lê-me!». The friends read it, retell it, and change it. At the end they write their own adventure and put it back in the library for other readers.

- **Big question:** *O que faz uma boa história?* (personagens · espaço · tempo · ação · um problema · um fim)
- **Running thread:** a "História da semana" bookmark on each estante's first page.
- **End of unit:** the pupils' own adventure goes into the library.

## The six-page pattern for each estante
There is some freedom where the text needs it.

1. **p1 · Antes de ler + text (part 1).** Contents:
   - the «História da semana» bookmark;
   - a prediction task;
   - the big illustration;
   - the start of the text.
2. **p2 · Text (end)**, plus a small vignette and a word box (3–4 words).
3. **p3 · Compreender.** Covers Leitura and Educação literária exactly as the scheme states them.
4. **p4 · Oralidade.** The task exactly as the scheme states it, with a speaking frame, a peer-listening checklist and a fluency or clarity rubric.
5. **p5 · Gramática.** Discovery from the story first, then the rule box, then 2–3 graded tasks.
6. **p6 · Escrita.** A plan (graphic organiser), then the draft, then a revision checklist.

- **Difficulty stars** go on every task: {{s1}} {{s2}} {{s3}}.
- **Activity numbers** restart on each page.

## Content by estante, taken from the scheme
**2.1**
- **Oralidade:** tell a story with introduction, development and conclusion.
- **Leitura:** personagens, espaço, tempo, ação.
- **EL:** what the character wants, and how the story ends.
- **Escrita:** a short three-part narrative from a plan.
- **Gramática:** presente, pretérito perfeito and futuro do indicativo of *ser, ir, ter, fazer*; *antes, ao mesmo tempo, depois*.

**2.2**
- **Oralidade:** retell the tale to recommend it to a classmate.
- **Leitura:** a short traditional tale, read in full.
- **EL:** what repeats, and what is typical of the traditional tale (the formula «Era uma vez», repeated rhymes, the pattern of three helpers, the smallest one wins).
- **Escrita:** recreate the tale with a new ending, keeping the sequence.
- **Gramática:** possessive determiners (meu, teu, seu…), demonstrative determiners (este, esse, aquele…), numeral quantifiers.

**2.3**
- **Oralidade:** read a passage aloud and say why you chose it.
- **Leitura:** a complete Torrado tale.
- **EL:** the theme of the text, and one idea the story left you with.
- **Escrita:** a short opinion about the story, with one reason.
- **Gramática:** adverbs; degree (muito, mais… do que, o mais); augmentative and diminutive.

**2.5**
- **Oralidade:** describe the characters and the place.
- **Leitura:** read the whole book, in parts, with the teacher.
- **EL:** follow the action, identify the obstacle, and say what you felt.
- **Escrita:** a short narrative of an encounter between the cat and the swallow (a new scene of our own).
- **Gramática:** noun phrase and verb phrase; subject and predicate.

**2.6**
- **Oralidade:** listen to the tale and record two pieces of information and one inference.
- **Leitura:** the complete traditional tale.
- **EL:** compare its place and characters with those of a Portuguese tale (O Coelhinho Branco).
- **Escrita:** a short informative text about Cabo Verde with introduction, development and conclusion. Use fact cards.
- **Gramática:** word families; prefix and suffix.

**2.8**
- **Oralidade:** narrate a day you lived, in order.
- **Leitura:** a diary.
- **EL:** tell apart what the character did from what he felt.
- **Escrita:** a diary of several days, each with a date, an event and a feeling.
- **Gramática:** the connectors e, mas, porque, depois, então.

**2.9**
- **Oralidade:** present the adventure plan to the class.
- **Leitura:** a short adventure, read in full, silently and aloud.
- **EL:** the problem and how it is solved.
- **Escrita:** a planned and revised adventure (spelling and punctuation).
- **Gramática:** stressed and unstressed syllables; prosodic stress and written accent.

## Checked facts (use only these; do not invent)
**António Torrado**
- Born in Lisbon in 1939; died in Lisbon in 2021.
- More than 100 books, mostly for children.
- Grande Prémio Gulbenkian de Literatura para Crianças (1988, for his whole body of work).
- Retold many traditional tales.
- *Trinta por uma linha* is a collection of 30 stories, recommended for 3.º ano.
- Other titles: *Como se faz cor-de-laranja*, *O mercador de coisa nenhuma*.

**Jorge Amado**
- Brazilian writer, born in Bahia in 1912, died in 2001.
- He wrote *O Gato Malhado e a Andorinha Sinhá* in 1948, in Paris, as a present for his son João Jorge's first birthday. The text was lost and was found again years later. It was published in 1976, with watercolours by Carybé.

**The story (for the teacher, and for organisers without quotations)**
- In a park, "in the days when animals talked", a love grows between the Gato Malhado, whom everyone fears, and the Andorinha Sinhá.
- The story follows the **four seasons**: spring (the love begins), summer (short, like happiness), autumn (the separation, a letter delivered by the Pombo-Correio), winter (the swallow marries the Rouxinol).
- At the end the cat goes away down the narrow paths.
- The frame story: the Manhã (Morning) and the Tempo (Time).
- The obstacle: the other animals' prejudice (a cat and a swallow "cannot" be together).

**O Coelhinho Branco (Adolfo Coelho)**
- The rabbit goes to his garden to fetch cabbages for a *caldinho*. When he comes back, the Cabra Cabrês is in his house: «Eu sou a cabra cabrês, que te salto em cima e te faço em três».
- He asks the boi, the cão and the galo for help. All three say «Eu não vou lá, que tenho medo».
- The formiga rabiga helps: «Eu sou a formiga rabiga, que te tiro as tripas e furo a barriga». She goes in through the keyhole.
- Our adaptation: the goat runs away, and the rabbit and the ant make the soup together.

**Cabo Verde**
- An archipelago of 10 islands in the Atlantic, off West Africa; 9 are inhabited.
- The capital is Praia (Santiago island).
- Portuguese is the official language; Cabo Verdean Creole (crioulo) is spoken every day.
- The climate is dry and there is little rain.
- «Ti Lobo» and «Chibinho» are the classic characters of its tales: the greedy uncle wolf and the clever young goat.

**The tale «O Lobo, o Chibinho e a Tia Ganga»**
- Ti Lobo, thin and greedy, wants to know why Chibinho is so plump. The answer: the eggs of the Tia Ganga.
- At dawn, when she goes out, Chibinho opens her door with «Porta, abre-te!» and shuts it with «Porta, fecha-te!».
- Chibinho eats a little and leaves. Ti Lobo stays inside, stuffing himself.
- When the Tia Ganga comes back, Ti Lobo mixes up the magic words.
- Our version for children: Tia Ganga chases him out with a broom and he never comes back. There is no violence.
