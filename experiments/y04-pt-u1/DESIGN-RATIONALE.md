# Design rationale: *Rua das Palavras*, Unidade 1

## The big idea
**Everyday texts are things people use to get something done.** The unit is a single story set on a real Lisbon street, Rua do Limoeiro in Alfama. A letter without a stamp blows down the street. It carries a retired postman's idea: turn the old red postbox into a street library. That idea then needs an **invitation**, the opening becomes **news** on the radio and in the neighbourhood paper, and the dog's mischief becomes a **comic strip**. Each text type turns up because the plot needs it, never as an isolated worksheet genre. The driving question is *«Para que servem os textos que encontramos todos os dias?»* (What are the texts we meet every day for?). The unit ends with children using all four text types themselves in a class newspaper.

## Why this structure
The order is **function → structure → practice → own production**, repeated at each "stop" (paragem) on the unit map:
- **Before reading (pp. 5–6):** predicting from the title and illustration, which covers the Educação Literária goal.
- **Four text-type stops, two pages each:** the text first, shown as a real object, then activities.
- **Grammar workshop (pp. 15–18):** discovery first, rule second. The same three words said with three voices teach the three sentence types. A mirror layout teaches negation. A board game gives oral practice. A faulty draft by Tomás gives the punctuation work.
- **Writing workshop (pp. 19–20):** an annotated model letter giving an opinion (opinion / reason / example), a plan, a draft, and a self and peer checklist.
- **Project and self-assessment (pp. 21–22),** then two pages of teacher notes. The teacher notes map each activity to the AE (3.º ano), with the facto/opinião extension from 4.º ano, and include a session plan, the transcript and the answers.

Difficulty is shown with one to three stars on every task, so a mixed class can work from the same page. Activity numbering restarts on every page.

## Why this visual style
- **Illustration (v2, at the user's request): Beatrix Potter-style watercolour animals.** Fine pen-and-ink with transparent washes on cream paper, and vignettes that fade into the page. The characters are original: a rabbit, a field mouse in a wheelchair, a red squirrel, a terrier puppy, a badger and an old tortoise postman. Animal characters give 8–9-year-olds a warm, timeless world. Keeping the Lisbon setting (azulejos, calçada, the kiosk, the red postbox) keeps the world Portuguese. The page colour is matched to the paper colour of the illustrations, so the art and the layout read as one surface.
- **Every document is drawn as an object:** a letter on stationery, an invitation card, a newspaper with a masthead, comic panels and an airmail envelope. Children learn what the text *looks like* in the world.
- **Colour code:** one colour per text type (carta red, convite yellow, notícia blue, BD green), plum for grammar. The code runs through the tags, task numbers and folios.
- **Type:** Andika, a typeface designed for early readers, for body text at 14–15 pt. Fraunces for display, which sits well beside the Edwardian art. Patrick Hand for anything "handwritten".

## Risks taken
- **A single continuous story** instead of unrelated short texts. It is stronger for motivation, but every page now depends on the plot.
- **Rules come after discovery.** On pp. 15–16 the rule sits below the examples, not above them.
- **A full board game page** in a grammar section.
- **Real Lisbon geography, plus a Sophia de Mello Breyner Andresen title** (mentioned only, not quoted) in the model letter.

## What I would improve with more time
- A human voice recording for the two QR audios.
- A redrawn "?" and "!" character set by a human illustrator.
- A true two-page spread for the comic.
- An imperative-sentence mini-lesson if the scheme allows.
- A Unit 2 bridge page with real content.
- Proofing by a native primary teacher, and field-testing the timings.

## Honest weaknesses
1. The illustrations are AI-generated in Potter's *style*. Fine details (paws, the wheelchair spokes, faux-writing squiggles on papers) would still get notes from a picky picture-book art director. Commercial-use terms must be confirmed before print.
2. The narrative has animals and humans' things side by side (a dog as a pet among dressed animals). This is a Potter convention, but a teacher may want to name it for the class.
3. The QR links are correct and decode from the page, but they only work after the site is deployed. Today they return 404.
4. The teacher notes (pp. 23–24) are dense at 10 pt.
5. Page 12 (the newspaper) uses 12 pt body text, to look like a real newspaper. That is below the 14 pt used everywhere else.
6. The audio is synthetic.
7. The PDF is RGB from Chrome, not a PDF/X file with a CMYK profile. `unit1-print.pdf` has a 3 mm bleed but no crop marks, so a printer's preflight is still needed.
