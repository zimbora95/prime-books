Prime School Books — Publishing Standard

The house standard for every Prime School Books title, from Year 1 to Year 12. Standardised editions of individual books live here too.

Two collections, one publishing house

There are two collections: Standard A covers Years 1 to 6, and Standard B covers Years 7 to 12. Standard A uses a warm Beatrix Potter watercolour storybook style with earth colours. Standard B uses a sophisticated abstract geometric editorial style, also with earth colours. The boundary is fixed at the Year 6/7 divide. A book belongs 100% to one standard and never mixes the two.

Standard A 2.0 — Years 1 to 6

The reference is Year 1 Art & Design, with Beatrix Potter-style illustration. Its page architecture, Fredoka/Andika typography, colour-coded units and learning components now define the whole Primary collection. This replaces the old Primary font rules and Physical Education reference. It does not change Standard B.

The reference PDF has been measured and all 88 pages reviewed in contact sheets. Its current artwork has not yet been converted, and its legacy defects are not part of the standard. No book content was moved or deleted in this revision.

1. Preserve the design, replace the illustrative voice

Keep the clear teaching hierarchy, purposeful images, generous pupil workspaces, labelled activity panels, unit reflection and picture vocabulary. Narrative scenes and illustrative objects use original Beatrix Potter-style fine ink and transparent watercolour: friendly woodland animals, believable clothing, muted pigments and cream paper.

Keep authentic evidence authentic. Photographs of named artworks, real places and specimens remain where they teach something an invented picture cannot. Technical diagrams, mathematics, letterforms and colour wheels stay accurate. Labels remain live text. No stretched images, repeated hero scenes, generated logos or lettering.

“Beatrix Potter-style” is an art direction, not an attribution to Potter. Historical original images require individual source-edition and rights checks. Generated images need truthful production disclosure. Approve one cover, scene and picture-card pilot before a batch.

2. Measured page architecture

US Letter, 612 × 792 pt; even page count. Ordinary content sits inside x 50.25–561.75 and y 66–734.25. Unit teaching pages have a full-width 47 pt tinted header with a 1.8 pt unit-colour rule. Footer rule: y 742. Circular folio: 24 pt diameter, centred at (550,758). Covers have no folio; the imprint has its measured smaller variant.

The normal page heading is Fredoka SemiBold 24 pt at baseline 120.75; the kicker is 9.5 pt at baseline 83.25. Year 1 reading text begins at baseline 152.25. Unit openers, contents, imprint, welcome and back matter have distinct templates: do not force a running unit band onto every page or clone accidental PDF repair patches.

3. Typography across Primary

Fredoka SemiBold for display; Andika Regular/Bold for reading, Years 1–6. Poppins is reserved for covers and publisher matter. Caveat is optional for brief nonessential annotations, Gelasio for actual quotations. No accidental Helvetica, Verdana, Arial or Type3.

Year 1: Andika 16 pt / 21 pt leading — measured from the reference.

Year 2: Andika 16 pt / 21 pt leading.

Year 3: Andika 15 pt / 20 pt leading.

Year 4: Andika 14.5 pt / 19.5 pt leading.

Year 5: Andika 14 pt / 19 pt leading.

Year 6: Andika 13.5 pt / 18.5 pt leading.

Years 2–6 are adopted series defaults, not measurements from the Year 1 book. Preserve larger existing teacher-approved sizes. Never shrink text to fit, delete content or move it to another page without permission. Smaller captions and glossary definitions in the reference are observations, not blanket approval for small pupil text.

4. Colour and cover identity

Keep one named theme per unit. The measured starter themes are violet (#4A3173 / #6B4BA1 / #EFE6FA), rose (#96204A / #D6336C / #FCE4EC), terracotta (#8C3A12 / #C4561F / #FBE7D8) and blue (#0B3C85 / #1256B8 / #DEEAFB): deep heading, mid opener/rule/badge, light band. Extra units need an explicit theme map, not an automatic six-colour template.

The muted watercolour palette belongs to artwork; it does not silently replace all page colours. The year-colour cover stripe is a separate series identifier. Front cover: 34 pt stripe, real logo at (85,42)–(149,106), Poppins ExtraBold 33 pt subject, clear year and Student Manual, art slot (34,315)–(612,792). Fit new artwork without distortion.

The back uses PRIME SCHOOL PRESS, subject, short book-specific blurb, warm rounded INSIDE THIS BOOK card and publisher footer. Use distinct art. No invented ISBN, retail price, examination-board endorsement or decorative barcode.

5. Structure and reusable components

Cover → imprint → contents → welcome → how to use → preparation/safety → units → synthesis/portfolio → illustrated glossary → sources and credits → closing → back cover. The reference has contents on physical page 3 and welcome on page 4. Existing approved book orders are not silently rearranged. Answers, index, year review and watch/listen resources are added when the subject needs them, not to satisfy a fixed page count.

Each unit follows discover → model → practise → extend → reflect → recall. Reuse the opener, topic pills, concept/model page, Your turn workspace, extension, applied project, I can review and visual word bank. Adapt the learning tasks to each subject: not every book needs four “rooms”, an Art exhibition or sixteen topics.

Keep panel meanings distinct: topic tint for support, amber for facts, deep colour for challenge, light framed or dashed pupil workspaces. Preserve approved table styles and provide usable answer space. A compact unit word bank and a spacious final dictionary are different components, not inconsistent versions of one grid.

Years 1–2 use direct naming and large actions; Years 3–4 add comparison and process; Years 5–6 use more independent enquiry and analytical diagrams. The medium remains the same, with less decorative character art as pupils mature.

6. Do not replicate the reference defects

The contents currently lists Unit 3 at 42 rather than its opener at 41, Unit 4 at 57 rather than 56, portfolio at 74 rather than 73, word book at 78 rather than 77, and sources at 86 rather than 85. Extracted figure/text content includes legacy fallback fonts. Sparse continuation pages need teacher review, not automatic deletion or decoration.

The physical interior folio values are correct. The current illustration direction is not yet the requested Potter conversion. This makes the book a design reference, not a fully certified release.

7. Replication and quality gate

Inventory the target input and master; map every existing page to a component; preserve its content; approve a representative pilot; build through measured components; verify all pages and navigation; then synchronise the PDF, markdown, cover and catalogue.

.venv/bin/python tools/audit_standard.py <slug> checks trim, even extent, folio values and extracted font families. A clean automated result still requires manual approval for reading size, layout, content preservation, contents/index targets, artwork accuracy, rights, contrast, image resolution, actual QR scans and print proof.

The existing PE renderer is an implementation to adapt, not the authority: its old body sizes and ochre defaults must not silently become the new standard. No corpus-wide rebuild is authorised by updating this specification.

Standard B — Years 7 to 12

1. Cover

The skeleton is identical to Standard A — same logo position, same title block, same spine band — but the art is grown-up: bold abstract geometry (circles, arcs, arches, grids, podiums) combined with subject-specific objects such as a microscope for Science, a compass for Mathematics, or circuit forms for Computing.

The palette is earth-toned: cream, terracotta, ochre, sage, slate, charcoal. Each year carries its own signature accent colour. Objects sit naturally on surfaces; no floating elements. Lighting is soft and realistic, editorial rather than corporate.

Every cover is a new original composition within the system. When matching a sibling cover, extract its exact geometry rather than eyeballing it. The thumbnail test applies here too. The back cover follows the same distinct-art rule as Standard A.

2. Typography, year by year

Year 7: Noto Sans, body 12.5–13.5 pt.

Year 8: Noto Sans, body 12–13 pt.

Year 9: Noto Sans, body 11.5–12.5 pt.

Year 10: Noto Sans, body 11–12 pt.

Year 11: Noto Sans, body 11–12 pt.

Year 12: Noto Sans, body 10–11.5 pt.

Noto Sans is used for every Secondary book, no exceptions — one collection, one voice. A serif companion (Source Serif 4 or Noto Serif) is permitted only for long Humanities reading passages, never for body content.

Unit titles run roughly 24–32 pt in Years 7–8, easing to 22–30 pt by Years 10–12; section headings run 16–20 pt down to 12.5–16 pt. Captions sit at 9–11 pt. Line spacing is about 1.2–1.3 times the font size. Two-column layouts are allowed.

The same rule applies: never reduce font size to fit — edit, reorganise, or flow onto the next page.

3. Structure of the book

Standard B retains its existing sequence: cover, About This Book, title, imprint, how to use, contents, units, glossary, index, references, answers, publisher close and back cover. The Primary revision does not reorder Secondary books.

About This Book states the course purpose and how the book is used. How to Use This Book includes the box legend and the QR convention. Glossary and Index are standard equipment, verified against the real book. Every unit opener carries learning objectives and key vocabulary.

Secondary books add two back-matter elements that Primary handles lightly:

Answers (selected answers or full answer key, stated clearly on the page which it is). References and Further Reading, in a consistent citation style (author, title, publisher, year; URLs for web sources with the access date). Any quotation, map, data set or image taken from outside the book is credited at the point of use or in an image-credits line, so nothing is reproduced without acknowledgement.

4. Page numbers and navigation

Folios appear on all content pages, with colour following the page background on dark opener pages, and are audited after any structural change. Contents and index are maintained exactly as in Standard A. Running headers may carry unit and section names from Year 7 up, in the house style.

5. Educational furniture

Feature boxes in the pack palette: Get started, Scenario, Did you know, Challenge yourself. Your Turn exercise panels with answer lines. Key Vocabulary boxes. Numbered, labelled diagrams, rigorous maps and data presentation.

Practical work carries a safety note where the activity involves heat, chemicals, tools, or fieldwork; the note style is consistent across subjects.

Educational QR codes are standardised identically to Standard A — same look, same caption convention, same placement discipline — so the linked-media feature is instantly recognisable from Year 1 to Year 12.

6. Language and quality gate

British English, no em-dashes. The full QC gate applies before done: folios, contents, index, references, credits, overlapping elements, cover and catalogue sync.

Rules that override everything

One. Priority order is Readability, then Accessibility, then Learning, then Visual hierarchy, then Brand consistency, then Aesthetics.

Two. Never delete or move teacher-placed content without explicit permission.

Three. Consistency, not uniformity — every book must look like it belongs to Prime School Books without looking like a photocopy of its siblings. Same skeleton, own personality.

Four. The repo master PDF is the book; any visual change travels with its regenerated cover, catalogue entry, and markdown companion.

The font rule: Standard A (Years 1–6): Fredoka display + Andika reading. Standard B (Years 7–12): its existing Noto Sans specification remains unchanged.
