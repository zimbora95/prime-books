Prime School Books — Publishing Standard

The house standard for every Prime School Books title, from Year 1 to Year 12. Standardised editions of individual books live here too.

Two collections, one publishing house

There are two collections: Standard A covers Years 1 to 6, and Standard B covers Years 7 to 12. Standard A uses a warm Beatrix Potter watercolour storybook style with earth colours. Standard B uses a sophisticated abstract geometric editorial style, also with earth colours. The boundary is fixed at the Year 6/7 divide. A book belongs 100% to one standard and never mixes the two.

Standard A — Years 1 to 6

1. Cover

The Prime School logo sits top-left, aligned with the title's left edge. The title block sits below it on the cream page: subject title in large bold type, "Year N" beneath it, then the programme line ("Early Years" or "Primary") and "Student Manual", with a mustard divider bar under the subtitle. A mustard-gold spine band runs down the full left edge.

The bottom two-thirds of the cover is a soft watercolour woodland illustration in the Beatrix Potter style: animals wearing clothing, using human tools, in a warm muted palette. The art fades softly into the cream background with no hard edge, and clear empty cream space always remains above the artwork.

Every cover keeps this same skeleton; only the artwork and subject change. New art is always generated for each book, never reused, and scenes vary per subject. The title must remain readable at thumbnail size on a small catalogue card.

The back cover carries a distinct still-life illustration (never the front scene reused), a short warm blurb about the book, and the Prime School Books mark. No price, no barcode placeholder unless print distribution requires one.

2. Typography, year by year

Year 1: Lexend, body 17–18 pt.

Year 2: Lexend, body 16–17 pt.

Year 3: Noto Sans, body 15–16 pt.

Year 4: Noto Sans, body 14–15 pt.

Year 5: Noto Sans, body 13.5–14.5 pt.

Year 6: Noto Sans, body 13–14 pt.

Lexend applies to Year 1 and Year 2 only, because beginning readers need open, highly legible letterforms. From Year 3 onwards the book switches to Noto Sans and never switches back within Primary.

Heading sizes step down with age as well: unit titles go from roughly 26–32 pt in Years 1–2 to 22–28 pt in Years 5–6, and section headings from 20–24 pt to 16–20 pt. Headings use the same font family as the body, in SemiBold or Bold.

Captions sit 1–2 pt below body size. Line spacing tightens gradually with age: about 1.4 times the font size in Years 1–2, 1.35 in Years 3–4, and 1.3 in Years 5–6. Text is always left-aligned with a ragged right edge.

Never shrink text to fit a page — reduce the content, redistribute it, or continue onto the next page. Header and footer bands are protected zones that running content never enters.

3. Structure of the book

The pages follow this fixed order:

Front cover. About This Book. Title page. Copyright/Imprint. How to Use This Book (one page: what the boxes mean, how to work through a unit). Contents. Units, each with an opener and its content. Glossary. Index. References and Further Reading (where sources exist). Answers (when the book contains exercises). About Prime School Books. Back cover.

About This Book is 100–150 words specific to this book, ending with the recommended age range determined from the actual content — never a fixed default range.

The imprint uses the fixed publisher boilerplate (Prime School Books, imprint of Prime School International) with clearly marked placeholders. ISBNs are never invented. The imprint also carries: the AI illustration note when artwork is generated (worded for the illustrations only), an image-credit line where photographs or third-party art appear, and the edition line ("First Edition, 2026" — renumbered on genuine reprints).

The Contents page uses dotted leaders with right-aligned page numbers, and is rebuilt and verified against the real book after any page change.

The closing page carries the standard Prime School Books statement with the tagline: Read · Imagine · Explore · Discover.

4. Page numbers and navigation

Every content page carries a folio (page number equals physical index plus one). The front cover, dark unit openers, and the final page may be exempt. Folio colour flips for legibility on dark pages.

After inserting or deleting pages, folios, contents, and index must all be re-audited together; they drift independently.

Every unit opener shows its number and title in the same position so a pupil can flip to any unit instantly. A running header or footer may carry the unit name on content pages.

5. Educational furniture

Recurring labelled boxes appear where they help learning: Learn, Explore, Think, Try It, Did You Know?, Key Vocabulary, and Remember. Their look is fixed per book but their meaning is the same across the collection. Key words are shown as bold term plus regular definition. All tables share the green-header house style. Figures are numbered per unit (Figure 1.1, 1.2 …) and clearly labelled, age-appropriate.

Each unit ends the same way: a short review or check-your-understanding element, so pupils always know a unit has finished.

Educational QR codes are part of the standard, with a standardised look across the collection: a clean framed code with a short caption in the book's green beneath it ("Watch: …"), placed only in genuinely free space, never colliding with text or answers. Every QR points to teacher-approved content; links are checked before print.

Practical activities (science, art, cookery, anything with tools or materials) carry a short safety note or an adult-supervision marker where relevant.

Where a book uses icons or symbols (activity types, difficulty, audio), a small legend key appears once, early in the book.

Illustration style, characters, and palette stay consistent across the whole book.

6. Accessibility

Strong text-to-background contrast everywhere. Essential information is never carried by colour alone (colour-coded elements always also have a word or icon). Text is never placed over busy illustration. Key vocabulary is visually findable. These rules apply at every year level.

7. Language and quality gate

British English throughout. No em-dashes. Before a book is declared done: spelling check, folio audit, contents truth-check, index spot-check, reference-link check, no overlapping elements, no duplicated or missing pages, cover regenerated, catalogue and markdown companion updated.

Standard B — Years 7 to 12

1. Cover

The skeleton is identical to Standard A — same logo position, same title block, same spine band — but the art is grown-up: bold abstract geometry (circles, arcs, arches, grids, podiums) combined with subject-specific objects such as a microscope for Science, a compass for Mathematics, or circuit forms for Computing.

The palette is earth-toned: cream, terracotta, ochre, sage, slate, charcoal. Each year carries its own signature accent colour. Objects sit naturally on surfaces; no floating elements. Lighting is soft and realistic, editorial rather than corporate.

Every cover is a new original composition within the system. When matching a sibling cover, extract its exact geometry rather than eyeballing it. The thumbnail test applies here too. The back cover follows the same distinct-art rule as Standard A.

2. Typography, year by year

Year 7: Noto Sans, body 12.5–13.5 pt.

Year 8: Noto Sans, body 12–13 pt.

Year 9: Noto Sans, body 11.5–12.5 pt.

Year 10: Noto Sans, body 11–12 pt.

Year 11: Noto Sans, body 11–12 pt.

Year 12: Noto Sans, body 10–11.5 pt.

Noto Sans is used for every Secondary book, no exceptions — one collection, one voice. A serif companion (Source Serif 4 or Noto Serif) is permitted only for long Humanities reading passages, never for body content.

Unit titles run roughly 24–32 pt in Years 7–8, easing to 22–30 pt by Years 10–12; section headings run 16–20 pt down to 12.5–16 pt. Captions sit at 9–11 pt. Line spacing is about 1.2–1.3 times the font size. Two-column layouts are allowed.

The same rule applies: never reduce font size to fit — edit, reorganise, or flow onto the next page.

3. Structure of the book

The page order matches Standard A, with secondary expectations:

About This Book states the course purpose and how the book is used. How to Use This Book includes the box legend and the QR convention. Glossary and Index are standard equipment, verified against the real book. Every unit opener carries learning objectives and key vocabulary.

Secondary books add two back-matter elements that Primary handles lightly:

Answers (selected answers or full answer key, stated clearly on the page which it is). References and Further Reading, in a consistent citation style (author, title, publisher, year; URLs for web sources with the access date). Any quotation, map, data set or image taken from outside the book is credited at the point of use or in an image-credits line, so nothing is reproduced without acknowledgement.

4. Page numbers and navigation

Folios appear on all content pages, with colour following the page background on dark opener pages, and are audited after any structural change. Contents and index are maintained exactly as in Standard A. Running headers may carry unit and section names from Year 7 up, in the house style.

5. Educational furniture

Feature boxes in the pack palette: Get started, Scenario, Did you know, Challenge yourself. Your Turn exercise panels with answer lines. Key Vocabulary boxes. Numbered, labelled diagrams, rigorous maps and data presentation.

Practical work carries a safety note where the activity involves heat, chemicals, tools, or fieldwork; the note style is consistent across subjects.

Educational QR codes are standardised identically to Standard A — same look, same caption convention, same placement discipline — so the linked-media feature is instantly recognisable from Year 1 to Year 12.

6. Language and quality gate

British English, no em-dashes. The full QC gate applies before done: folios, contents, index, references, credits, overlapping elements, cover and catalogue sync.

Rules that override everything

One. Priority order is Readability, then Accessibility, then Learning, then Visual hierarchy, then Brand consistency, then Aesthetics.

Two. Never delete or move teacher-placed content without explicit permission.

Three. Consistency, not uniformity — every book must look like it belongs to Prime School Books without looking like a photocopy of its siblings. Same skeleton, own personality.

Four. The repo master PDF is the book; any visual change travels with its regenerated cover, catalogue entry, and markdown companion.

The one-line font rule: Lexend for Years 1–2, then Noto Sans from Year 3 to Year 12, with body size stepping down at every year according to the lists above.
