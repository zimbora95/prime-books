How this book works

Welcome to Prime Books Mathematics for Year 9. This book is written for you, the pupil, to read, argue with and write
in. It is not a book to be afraid of. Every idea is introduced with a reason before it becomes a rule, and every rule is
followed by the chance to use it until it feels obvious.

add, subtract, multiply and divide. In this first unit you will stretch those four operations across the whole number
line, into the territory of the negatives, and then use them to unlock older and deeper patterns: multiples, factors,

quick tests for divisibility, and the roots that undo powers.

UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.1

Irrational numbers

Learn

A rational number can be written as a fraction of two integers, such as 3/4, 7 or 0.5. Every rational number, written
as a decimal, either ends or repeats. Some numbers do neither. √2 = 1.41421356… goes on for ever without

repeating, and can never be written exactly as a fraction. Numbers like this are irrational. So are √3, √5 and the
famous constant π.

✦
G E T STA RT E D

Write 84 as a product of prime factors.
1

Work out: a. 3⁴ b. 2⁶ c. (−2)³.
2

Work out, giving your answer as a power: a. 5³ × 5² b. 7⁸ ÷ 7⁵.
3

Is √25 rational or irrational? Explain.
4

◆
S C E N A R I O

The astrophysicists at the European Southern Observatory measure the distance to a star in kilometres
and the diameter of an atom in metres. One number is staggeringly large, the other unimaginably small,
yet both are written neatly using the same idea: powers of ten. This unit is about taming the very large, the

very small, and the numbers that refuse to be written as fractions.

∎
W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 .1

Say whether each number is rational or irrational: a. √16 b. √2 c. 0.75

a. √16 = 4, which is an integer: rational
b. √2 = 1.41421356… never ends or repeats: irrational
c. 0.75 = 3/4, a fraction: rational

Why: A number is rational exactly when it can be written as a fraction of two integers, which means its

decimal ends or repeats.

UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.2

Standard form

Learn

Standard form writes a number as A × 10ⁿ, where A is at least 1 but less than 10, and n is an integer. It is how
scientists handle the very large and the very small without drowning in zeros. So 4500 = 4.5 × 10³, and the tiny

0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴.

∴
T H I N K L I K E A M AT H E M AT I C I A N

The ancient Greeks were shocked to discover that √2 is irrational. Legend says Hippasus was drowned for
revealing it. A proof goes like this: suppose √2 = a/b in lowest terms, and chase the consequences until both
a and b must be even, which is a contradiction. Why does "both must be even" break the assumption "in

lowest terms"? Discuss.

UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.3

Indices

Learn

You know that 5⁰ = 1 and that indices add and subtract. Now meet two more ideas. A negative index means a

reciprocal: 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8. A fractional index means a root: 9(1/2) = √9 = 3, and 8(2/3) = (∛8)² = 4.

∎
W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 . 2

Work out (3.2 × 10⁴) × (2 × 10³), giving your answer in standard form.

multiply the front numbers: 3.2 × 2 = 6.4
add the indices: 10⁴ × 10³ = 10⁷
so the answer is 6.4 × 10⁷

Why: The front numbers multiply and the powers of ten combine by adding indices, exactly as the laws of
indices promise.

**●**

3

∎
W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 . 3

Work out: a. 4^(3/2) b. 5⁻² c. 8⁰

a. 4^(3/2) = (√4)³ = 2³ = 8
b. 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25
c. 8⁰ = 1

Why: A fractional index splits into a root and a power; a negative index flips to a reciprocal; anything to the
power zero is one.

GLOSSARY

Unit 1

The mathematical words this unit has taught you, gathered in one place.

common factor: a number that is a factor of two or more numbers.

common multiple: a number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

consecutive: following one after another in order.

cube number: a number made by multiplying an integer by itself twice, such as 27 = 3³.

cube root: the number that cubes to give the value under the root, as in ∛27 = 3.

digit: any of the symbols 0 to 9 used to write numbers.

divisible: able to be divided exactly, with no remainder.

factor: a number that divides another exactly.

highest common factor (HCF): the largest common factor of two or more numbers.

index: the small raised number showing a power, as in 5².

integer: a positive or negative whole number, or zero.

inverse: the number that undoes another; the inverse of 4 is −4.

inverse operation: an operation that reverses another; subtraction is the inverse operation of addition.

lowest common multiple (LCM): the smallest common multiple of two or more numbers.

multiple: the result of multiplying a number by a positive integer.

number line: a line on which numbers are placed in order.

product: the result of a multiplication.

quotient: the result of a division.

square number: a number made by multiplying an integer by itself, such as 36 = 6².

square root: the number that squares to give the value under the root, as in √36 = 6.

test for divisibility: a quick rule that tells you whether one number divides another without doing the division.
