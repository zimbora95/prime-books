## Introduction

This book is a studio you can carry. It has four working tables, and each

one is a unit: a drawing table, a sculpt table, a craft table and a paint

table.

In earlier years you looked carefully, printed, painted, drew depth and

built simple structures. Keep all of it. This year the work asks for

## something new: control and choice. Which tool, which material, which

colour, which feeling, and why that one.

Every unit works the same way, so you always know where you are.

At the back of the book there is a studio show to aim for, an assessment

page, the answers to the closed questions, a word list, the sources used,

and notes for teachers.

A unit opener says what the unit is about and what you will be able to

do.

Each topic starts with a question to talk about, then a short

explanation.

Panels tell you when to watch, when to make, and when to look back at

your work.

Dashed boxes are yours. Draw, plan, stitch a sample or paint in them.

A review at the end of each unit asks what you can do now.

**Assessment**

Evidence, not marks: one kept piece per unit and a one-minute spoken

account of it. The Studio assessment page carries the criteria in pupil

language, and the Answers section gives acceptance criteria for every

open task, so two teachers can agree on what a finished piece looks like.

**Safety**

Needles, cutting tools and clay dust follow school policy. Blunt needles for

all stitching, counted out and counted back in. Clay tools are blunt; wipe

surfaces rather than sweeping dry clay. Any outdoor painting needs a plan

agreed in advance.

**Differentiation**

Shorter route: fewer mark families, a modelled thumbnail, one stitch

instead of two.

Longer route: the Go a bit further and Big challenge panels, then a

second purpose painting for a real request from another class.

Pupils who dislike open making are often unlocked by the purpose

questions in topic 4.3: a job for somebody else is easier to start than a

free choice.
