The potting shed, early, before anyone has come in.

At the top of the garden there is an old wooden shed. Inside there is a

Six friends work in this shed. They grow things and mend things. They

That is what a scientist does. You are going to do it too.

## A habitat is the place where an animal or a plant lives. A good
habitat gives it everything it needs.

Every living thing needs food and water. It needs air. It needs a
safe place to rest.

The log pile is a habitat. It is dark and damp under the bark, and a
woodlouse likes that. The wall is a habitat too, but it is dry and
sunny. A woodlouse would soon dry up there.

## So a habitat is not just any place. It is the right place.

People change the environment. Some changes help, and some do
harm.

✎For teachers

Pupils at this age readily accept that a habitat is a place, and less readily that it is the
RIGHT place. The woodlouse test in Topic 1.1 is the argument that carries it: same
garden, two stones, different results. Repeat it outdoors if a pupil is unconvinced.

Topic 1.4's dust test is the book's first fair test. The point is not which rock wins, it is
that the number of rubs was the same for each. Ask pupils what would happen if one
rock had been rubbed twice as much.

## A shadow forms when something blocks the light.

Stand in the sunshine and your body blocks the light. A dark shape
falls on the ground beside you.

## Shadows do not stay the same all day. They change in length and in
## direction.

In the early morning shadows are long. Near the middle of the day
they are short.

In the evening they grow long again, and they point the other way.

## The Sun appears to move across the sky. That is why our shadows
swing slowly round.

The Earth is the thing that really moves. It travels round the Sun,
and it spins once each day.

So watch the shadows on the ground. They tell you the story safely.

## A circuit is a loop. The electricity runs all the way round it.

## You need three things to light a bulb. You need a battery, two wires
## and a bulb.

Join the battery to the bulb with the two wires. Now the loop is

closes again.

SCIENCE SKILLS

Being fair, and saying what you found

## A test is fair when you change one thing only. In Unit 3 you dripped the
## same water onto every square. In Unit 4 you gave all five beans the
## same drink.

If you had watered one bean more, you could not say what made it
grow.

What we did

We poured the same water onto each of the six
squares.

What we saw

When you have finished, say what you found. Say it plainly, and say how
you know. If it surprised you, say that too.

A scientist who says "I do not know yet" is telling the truth. The truth is where the
next question comes from.

✎For teachers

The fair-test idea is the single most transferable thing in this book and the hardest at
this age. Pupils will happily change two variables and then argue about which one
mattered. When that happens, ask them to run it again changing only one, and let the
disagreement do the teaching.

Return to this page whenever a pupil says a result is unfair. The vocabulary here is
deliberately the vocabulary of Units 3 and 4, so they can point at their own work as
the example.

About this book

a modelled enquiry, at least fourteen things to do, and a "How do you know?" prompt.

**Safety. Practical work assumes direct adult supervision. Carry out your own risk assessment first and**
follow your school policy. Two rules are worth repeating aloud at every session rather than reading
once: never look straight at the Sun (Unit 5), and only an adult may plug in or unplug a mains
appliance, because mains electricity can kill (Unit 6). All pupil circuit work in this book uses a battery,
and no activity anywhere goes near a socket.

**Misconceptions this book tackles head on. That the Moon makes its own light (Unit 5, where it is**
named as a reflector). That the Sun moves round the Earth (Unit 5 teaches the Sun's APPARENT
movement, through shadows on the ground). That a force is only ever a push (Unit 2 gives pulling
equal time, with twine the pupil feels in her own paws). That a fair test can change two things at once
(Unit 3, where the disagreement is left to do the teaching). In each case the wrong idea is named, put
in a character's mouth, and then tested, because a misconception that is never spoken aloud is never
given up.
