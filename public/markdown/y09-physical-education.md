## 1.1 Introduction and rules

Volleyball is a net game of six players. A rally is won when the ball lands in the opponents' court or they
commit a fault. Modern school matches use rally-point scoring: every rally scores, whoever served.

A team has three contacts to send the ball back. The usual pattern is pass, set, attack. A player may not
hit the ball twice in a row except after a block. The ball may touch any part of the body.

Year 9 adds rotation discipline. After you win the serve you rotate clockwise. Standing in the wrong
place is a fault, even if the hit looked good.

Volleyball: centre line and attack lines (3 m from the net)

**KEY POINTS**

Six players. Three contacts. Rally-point scoring.

Rotate clockwise when you win the serve.

Net touch, foot over the centre line and a double hit are common faults.

The ball is in if it touches the line.

## 1.3 Ball control

The forearm pass is the first contact after most serves. Platform together, arms straight, legs do the
work. The ball should rise in a friendly arc to the setter, not shoot off toward the sideline.

The overhead set uses the fingertips, not the palms. Meet the ball above the forehead, extend the legs,
and finish with the hands toward the target. A set that is too low forces a weak attack.

Digging is a low forearm pass when the attack is already coming down. Get the platform under the ball.
A spectacular dive that misses is worse than a simple step.

A forearm pass is a platform. The legs aim the ball; the arms stay quiet.

**KEY POINTS**

Platform: thumbs together, elbows long.

Set: fingertips, not a slap.

The pass is successful when the setter can play it.

Move your feet first. The arms only finish the job.

## 1.4 Attacking skills

Attacking in Year 9 is more than a big swing. Approach, jump, contact and land. The last two steps are
quick-quick. Contact the ball in front of the hitting shoulder, high, with a firm wrist.

A tip is a soft attack when the block is up. A roll shot drops just over the block. The best attackers in this
year can choose, not only smash.

Serving starts the rally. An underhand serve is legal and useful. An overhand serve adds speed. A jump
serve is a Year 9 option only when the toss is consistent.

A spike is timed, not wild. High contact and a two-foot landing protect the next rally.

**KEY POINTS**

Approach, jump, high contact, two-foot landing.

Tip and roll are attacks, not admissions of defeat.

Serve to a space, not to the best passer.

Do not attack if a simple set would be better.

## 1.5 Defensive skills

Blocking is a defensive attack. Watch the setter, then the hitter's shoulders. Hands reach over the net
without touching it. A well-timed block takes away the sharp cross-court line.

Back-court defence starts in base: knees bent, hands ready, eyes on the hitter. After the block, uncover
the space the block does not cover.

Reading is a Year 9 skill. A hitter who opens the shoulders toward the line is likely to hit the line. Move
before the contact, not after the bounce.

A block is a wall with timing. Hands reach over the net and the feet land ready to play the next ball.

**KEY POINTS**

Hands over the net, not on it.

Base first, then the extra step.

The uncovered angle is your job.

Talk: line, cross, deep, short.

## 1.6 Game strategies

A simple Year 9 offence is pass-set-hit with the setter in the front row or coming from the back. A 4-2
uses two setters. A 6-6 lets anyone set if they are in the right place. Choose one system and keep it for a
match.

On defence, a 2-1-3 or a player-up / player-back shape is enough. The point is that everyone knows
who takes the tip and who takes the deep ball.

Rotation is a tactic, not only a rule. Hide a weaker passer in a quieter serve-receive seat. Put your best
blocker opposite the other team's best hitter when you can.

**KEY POINTS**

One offensive system per match.

Name who takes the tip and who takes deep.

Serve-receive is a planned shape.

Talk before the serve, not after the fault.

Korfball is a mixed invasion game invented in the Netherlands. A team has four women and four men,
split into two zones. You may only shoot if you are free: a defender with an arm raised within arm's
reach makes the shot defended and therefore illegal.

three rules create the sport: movement without the ball is everything.

Goals are scored by throwing the ball down through the korf. After two goals the teams swap zones
and often swap attack and defence roles.

Korfball: two zones, one korf in each. Attackers may not enter the other zone

**KEY POINTS**

Mixed teams. Two zones. One korf each.

No running with the ball.

No shot if you are defended.

Roles swap after two goals.

## Word list

These words are used in the units. The definition matches the way the book uses the word.

**Attack line**
The line 3 m from the net in volleyball. Back-row players may not attack a high ball in front of
it.

**Base**
The ready place you return to after a contact or a shot.

**Clear**
A high, deep badminton shot that pushes the opponent back.

**Defended**
**shot**

In korfball, a shot taken when a defender is within arm's reach with an arm raised. It does not
count.

**Down**
One play in American football. In this course it ends on a two-hand touch or an incomplete
pass.

**Fartlek**
A run where the ground or a simple rule tells you when to lift the pace.

**Korf**
The basket on top of the korfball post.

**Platform**
The flat forearm surface used to pass a volleyball.

**Rally-point**
A scoring system in which every rally scores, whoever served.

**Rotation**
In volleyball, moving one place clockwise when you win the serve.

**Route**
The path a receiver runs in American football.

**Set**
An overhead volleyball pass that prepares an attack.

**Smash**
A steep downward badminton hit from a high shuttle.

**Spiral**
An American football throw that spins around its long axis so it flies true.

**Split step**
A small hop as the opponent hits, so you can move in any direction.

**Touch**
**football**

The version of American football used in this book: a two-hand touch ends the play.

**Transition**
The movement that joins two steps or two shapes in a routine.
