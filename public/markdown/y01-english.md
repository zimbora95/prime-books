WELCOME
This book is a trail

A trail is a path you walk. This one goes all the way through the year,

## and there are nine stops along it. At every stop something happens: a

bus comes, a rocket goes up, a bun runs away, a penguin looks for his

friends.

You do not have to read on your own yet. That is what the pictures are

for. Every new word in this book comes with its own picture, so you can

always read the page.

★Did you know?

## Your brain learns a new word best when you say it out loud. So

say every new word in this book at least three times: once to

yourself, once to a friend, and once as loudly as you dare.

FOR TE ACHERS
How this book is built

✎How to use this book with your class

**Part One comes first for a reason. The sequence used in many Year 1 courses**
begins reading connected text before a five-year-old has secure letter
knowledge. Part One inserts twenty-three pages of foundation work ahead of
that: the five vowels individually, first consonants, and the ten highest-
frequency words, each with formation practice.

**Every new sound gets its own page before it is needed. Each of the nine**
**stops opens its phonics work with a Meet the letter page: balloon letter,**
formation arrows, a sound tree of picture words, then handwriting. Pupils
meet the letter, make the letter, and only then read it in a story.

**No answers are printed anywhere. Pupils say what they think out loud, then**
circle, tick, colour or write. This is deliberate: a printed answer turns a
thinking task into a copying task.

**Every word prints with its own picture. No picture is reused for a different**
word, because a nearly-right picture teaches the wrong thing.

**Children copy words, not sentences, through Part One and into the early**
stops. One tramline per word, with the picture beside it.
