## About this book

This is a book of letters. Six children live a long way from you, in six
different countries, and this year they write to tell you about their
games, their homes, their journeys and the days they look forward to
most.

You write back. There is a space on the page for your answer, and a
pencil is the only thing you need to start.

## The book is called Dear World, and it has six units. Each one is a
colour. When you find that colour at the top of a page, you know
which unit you are in.

**One promise**

## In every unit there is one big question. Its answer is not printed
anywhere in this book, and that is on purpose. Those are the
questions worth talking about, with your class, at home, or with the
six children who write to you.

## Welcome to Dear World

Somewhere today, a child you have never met is doing something you
would recognise straight away. Kicking a ball. Helping to carry the
shopping. Waiting for a bus. Laughing at a joke you would probably
laugh at too.

They are also doing something you might never have seen. Playing a
game with seeds and a wooden board. Walking past a house made of
clay. Sending a message to a teacher who lives four hundred
kilometres away.

Both things are true at the same time. That is what this book is
about.

**Look at the globe. Six children are posting a picture to somebody far away. Find the letter**
slot.

**How the post arrives**

Every unit opens with a letter. Read it first. It tells you what one of
the six children has been doing, and it always ends with a question

You answer on the page, in pencil, in your own words. There is no

different. Which do you think matters more?

## TIME CAPSULE

**History**

Long before televisions, phones or computers, children were already
playing. People were carving boards for games like mancala and senet
out of stone and wood thousands of years ago, and some of those
boards are still here for us to see today. Skipping, chasing and ball
games are just as old. The games in this unit are older than your
grandparents, older than your town, and one day the children of the
future will play them too.

## For teachers

**What this book is**

An independent Prime Books student book for Year 1 Global
Perspectives, written to the school's 2026-27 scheme of work: six
units, 58 topics. It is not published by, endorsed by or affiliated with
any examination board.

**How a unit is shaped**

Every unit opens with a letter from one of six children, in Ghana,
Brazil, India, Australia, Mexico and the Netherlands. Each topic then
runs: teaching, sometimes a worked example or a second way of
seeing, then a task with room to write or draw.

## The big question appears once per unit and has no answer printed
## anywhere in this book. That is deliberate, and it is printed on the
back cover as a promise to the reader. It is there to be talked about,
not marked.

**Assessment**

Nothing in this book is a test. How did it go? is a self-check the pupil
colours in, and Now I can lists each unit's outcomes in the pupil's
own language. The Answers section exists so that a teacher and a
pupil can check closed questions together.

**Two topics that need a word before you teach them**

## Unit 2 is called Rich and poor, which is the scheme's own title.
Nothing in it asks a pupil to say what their family has or has not. The
teaching is about what every person needs and how communities
share, and the sorting tasks use objects, never classmates.
