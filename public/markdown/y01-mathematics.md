ABOUT THIS BOOK · FOR TEACHERS

✎For teachers

This book is meant to be written in. Pupils will draw rings, tick boxes, join pairs with a
line, colour squares and write numerals on these pages, and that is the point.

pupils say what they think out loud first, then write.

How do we measure?

☘Choose the tool that fits the question

Every question has its own tool. To find out which is heavier, use
## balance scales. To find out how long, use a ruler. To find out how
## much a jug holds, fill it with cups.

Hands and cups work, but my hand is not the same size as yours,
so our numbers disagree. That is why everybody uses the same
## units: the centimetre and the metre for length, the litre for how
## much a jug holds, and the kilogram for mass.

✎For teachers

**There are no printed answers in this book. The single exception is the Watch me do**
**one panel, which opens every topic with one example solved in full, step by step,**
before pupils try one of their own. Everywhere else the page is left for the pupil's own
work, and the answer belongs in the discussion, not in the margin.

This book is meant to be written in. A dashed line, a dashed box or a dotted frame is
always the pupil's space: to write a numeral, draw counters, ring a choice, join a pair
or colour a square. Practical work comes first, and the material to fetch is listed on
**the page under The material you need.**

**Every topic asks How do you know? at least once, and that question carries the**
mathematics of Year 1. Accept the explanation in the pupil's own words: pointing at
each thing in turn, counting again out loud from the other end, pairing counters up,
lining two sets side by side or moving the objects across the table are all good
answers. Ask it again in the corridor, at the sand tray and over the fruit bowl, until
saying how you know becomes a habit.

**Each unit closes with Now I can and a self-assessment table. Its three columns are**
**worded confident, almost there and needs more work. Please keep that wording**
when you talk the table through: it names what a pupil is going to do next, which a
colour cannot, and no pupil has to be told that a colour means they were wrong.
Pupils tick their own boxes.

**The six Along the way projects and the final Counting fair need a table, a partner**
and about a lesson each. They are the places where the year's mathematics leaves
the page, so they are worth the room they take.
