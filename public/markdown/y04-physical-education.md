The Sports Day Field

A student book for Lower Primary, written for pupils of about
eight to nine years old. Year 1 played in the meadow. Year 2

Welcome to the sports day field

Last year you learned the most useful thing in this whole subject: how to take one
movement you could already do and make it better on purpose.

This year you are going to find out what happens when you have to do that with
everything at once, on a field with other people on it.

Here is the difference, and it is a real one. On the Cinder Track in Year 3, one body
moved at a time and two friends watched it. On the sports day field, four events run
together. Somebody is throwing behind you. Somebody is coming down the next lane.
The person who set out your station has just moved a cone while you were looking the
other way.

None of that makes the movement harder. It makes keeping the movement harder,
and that is the Year 4 job.

The six friends walk out. Rowan has the hoops, Bramble has the cones, Tuft has a clipboard almost as
big as he is, Sorrel has the measuring tape and Pip has the baton. Willow is flying low behind them.
Nobody is running yet, and everything in this book is being carried onto the field in this picture.

Tuft

Willow

Barn owl. She watches, from up on a post where she can see the whole shape of
a movement and the whole field at the same time. She is the reason this book

Rowan

Otter. Happiest in a group, very good at giving

exactly how a class works, and it is why the tasks keep making you swap.

**○**
COME OUT TO THE FIELD

Think about October, at the start of this school year.

Now answer the harder question. What could you do then that you have not
done since?

Almost everybody has something. A movement does not disappear, but it goes
quiet, and a quiet movement is slower and wobblier than it used to be. This unit
is about finding out which of yours have gone quiet, and about waking them up
without losing the rest.

▣
THE EQUIPMENT YOU NEED

Eight cones, four bean bags, two hoops, one low hurdle, a stopwatch and a pencil. Work in fours.

✦
WARM UP

Travel anywhere in the space for ninety seconds, changing how you travel every
time your teacher calls: walking, side-stepping, skipping, jogging. Never touch
anybody and never stop.

Then five shoulder rolls backwards, five rises onto your toes, and stand still. Your
body is warm and your ankles are awake. Cold practice is wasted practice.

DID YOU KNOW?

The hardest event in athletics is not one event. The decathlon is ten of them,
held over two days, and the order never changes. Day one: 100 metres, long
jump, shot put, high jump, 400 metres. Day two: 110 metres hurdles, discus, pole
vault, javelin, 1500 metres.

Read that list again and notice what it asks for. Sprinting, then jumping, then
throwing, then sprinting again the next morning, on tired legs.

A decathlete’s problem is never how to get good at one of the ten. It is how to
keep all ten at once, which is exactly the problem on this page.

Timing

Four topics and a project fill about six lessons of 45 minutes, which matches the Input,

rushed lesson drops.

Assessment

Assess from the Look what I can do list and from step 5 of the project, not from the
times. Two specific traps:

The Input names three assessment points across the year, one per term. Those

A pupil who is quick and hesitates three times has not met 1.3. A pupil who is

repeatable. Ask for it twice, back to back, every time.
