## Welcome to the Bright Studio

There is a bright room with one long table.

On the table you will find pencils, soft charcoal sticks, tiny paper tiles, brushes
standing in a jar, and a pile of card ready to fold.

## This book is that room. It is called the Bright Studio, and it belongs to you for this
year.

You do not need to be "good at art" to start. You need curious eyes and hands that
want to try.

Four kinds of making live here:

Rufus the fox works carefully with clay and line. Bruno the bear mixes colour
slowly and never minds starting again. You will see both of them as you go.

## Here is how it works

## Observational drawing means drawing from something real that is sitting in
front of you, right now.

It is not one action. It is a loop, and the loop is the whole craft.

Most people do step 1 for one second and step 2 for ten minutes. Artists do the
opposite.

## Here is how it works

## A sketch is a quick drawing that keeps a moment or an idea. It is not finished art,
and it is not meant to be. It is meant to be honest and useful.

Four habits make sketches worth keeping:

Write the date on the page.
**1**

Draw for three to five minutes, not for thirty.
**2**

Add three words about what you noticed.
**3**

Keep the page even when you dislike it. Sketchbooks are not for showing off.
**4**

**DI D YO U K N O W ?**

## The word sketch came into English from the Italian word schizzo, which
means a rough, quick drawing. Artists have kept sketchbooks for hundreds
of years, and most of those pages were never meant for anyone else to see.
