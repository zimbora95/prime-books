**WELCOME**

## This is your studio

Six pairs of painted hands, and not one of them minded getting messy. This is what a good art lesson
looks like at the end.

A studio is a room where people make things. Painters have studios,
and so do people who sew, build and draw. This book is a studio too,
and it belongs to you. You do not need to be good at art to start: you
## only need to be curious. Every artist in the world began by making a
mark and looking at what happened.

**Four rooms, four ways to make**

## In the Mark Room you draw. In the Thread Room you weave
## and sew. In the Clay Room you build shapes you can hold. In
## the Paint Room you mix colours and paint big.

**Did you know?**

People have been making art for a very, very long time. In a
cave called Chauvet in France, there are paintings of animals
## that are about 32,000 years old. That is older than every city
on Earth.

## The oldest drawings of all

Long before anyone had paper, people drew on the walls of caves.
They pressed their hands against the rock and blew colour around
them, leaving a hand shape behind. You can still see those hands
today.

**Figure 1.5 Hand shapes on the wall of Cueva de las Manos in Argentina, whose name means Cave of**
**the Hands. The oldest paintings there were made about 9,300 years ago. Photograph: Wikimedia**
Commons.

You can try it safely with a pencil: put your hand flat on paper and draw all the way round it.

**Did you know?**

Cave artists did not have brushes. They chewed colour and
water together and sprayed it from their mouths around their
hand, like a very old spray can.

## Over, under, over, under

Weaving needs two sets of threads. One set waits, stretched tight.
The other set travels across, going over one thread and under the
next.

**warp: the threads that wait**

**weft:**
the thread
that travels

Over, under, over, under.
The next row starts the other way round.

**Figure 2.1 Plain weave. Follow the rose thread with your finger: over, under, over, under. The next**
row starts the other way round.

**Figure 2.2 A loom you can make from card.**

**Figure 2.3 A real wooden loom, about 200 years**
old. Photograph: Wikimedia Commons.

**Look and learn**

## The threads that wait are called the warp. The thread that
## travels is called the weft. Weft goes left to right, like reading. If
## you say "warp waits, weft travels" a few times, you will not
forget.

from somewhere real. Here is where, so an adult can look it up with
you.

**The facts we checked**

**Page 04, Chauvet Cave. The painted animals in the Chauvet-Pont-d'Arc cave in the**
Ardeche, France, are dated to roughly 32,000 to 30,000 years ago. Chauvet Cave,
Wikipedia, accessed 29 July 2026. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvet_Cave

**Page 14, ochre. Ochre is a family of natural clay earth pigments made of ferric oxide**
with clay and sand, ranging from yellow to deep orange or brown. Ochre, Wikipedia,
accessed 29 July 2026.

**Page 21, Cueva de las Manos. The hand stencils in Santa Cruz, Argentina, were**
made in several waves; the earliest rock art at the site dates to about 7,300 BC,
roughly 9,300 years ago. Cueva de las Manos, Wikipedia, accessed 29 July 2026.
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_de_las_Manos

**Page 35, Arraiolos. Embroidered wool rugs have been made in Arraiolos, Portugal,**
since at least the 16th century, and by the late 17th century they appear in Lisbon
customs records. Arraiolos, Wikipedia, accessed 29 July 2026.

**Page 52, Alexander Calder. Calder built Cirque Calder, a miniature circus of wire,**
cloth, string and cork, from 1926 onwards, and performed with it himself. Alexander
Calder, Wikipedia, accessed 29 July 2026.

**Page 67, azulejos. The blue of Portuguese tin-glazed tiles comes from cobalt, a**
pigment that reached Europe along trading routes from China and Persia. Azulejo,
Wikipedia, accessed 29 July 2026.

## Four rooms. One studio. Yours.

## Open the door of The Mark Room and make every mark your hand
## can make. Cross the hall to The Thread Room and weave a thread
## over and under until it becomes cloth. In The Clay Room build
## something solid you can hold and turn round. Then push open The
## Paint Room and discover that three colours are enough to make
them all.

Along the way you will meet cave artists who painted round their
own hands, rug makers who sewed for a whole year, a sculptor
whose art hangs in the air, and the deep blue tiles that cover the
walls of Portugal.
