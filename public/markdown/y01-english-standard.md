This is your reading book. Six animals read here with you, and every
one of them gets a word wrong at least once. Wren says a new word

## Four good reading habits

We say them out loud at the start of every lesson.

## 1
## Point as you read
Your finger keeps your eyes in the right place. Move
it under each word as you say it.

## 2
## Say it out loud
Ears learn faster than eyes do. Say every new word
three times, then say it in a sentence.

## 3
## Look at the picture first
The picture tells you what the words are about, so
you are never completely lost on a page.

## 4
## Stop and ask
A word you do not know is a good question, not a
mistake. Ask, and then keep reading.

4.1 Two jobs a word can do

## Doing words and naming words

Doing words tell you what somebody does: run, hop, jog, sit. Naming
words tell you what something is: box, mug, frog, mud. Say each
word, then ask yourself which job it does.

doing: run, hop, jog, sit

naming: box, mug, frog, mud

run is what your legs do

a mug is a thing you hold

ask: what is it doing, or what is it?

**Say this**
The words verb and noun can wait. What matters this year is the sorting: does the word tell
me an action, or name a thing?

**Unit 5 · Finding Out**
5.1 Two kinds of book

## A story book, and a fact book

A story book has characters who do things, and somebody made
them up. A fact book has real photographs, real facts, and a contents
page, so you can jump straight to what you want to know.

story book: characters, made up

fact book: photographs, true

fact book: a contents page helps you

a fox is real: a fact book tells you about it

a talking bun is made up: that is a story

**Say this**
Ask which kind of book would tell you how tall a penguin is, and which one you would want at
bedtime. Both answers are right.

## Five things we do every day

Not a test, and not a race. These five are what a reading lesson looks
like in this book.

## Point at the words
A finger under the words keeps your eyes in the right place,
especially on a new page.

## Say it out loud
Ears learn faster than eyes do. Read it once in your head,
then once out loud.

## Look at the picture first
The picture tells you what the words will be about before
you read a single one.

## Tell it back
Say the story again in your own words. That is how we know
you understood it.

## Ask for a word
A word you do not know is a good question, never a
mistake.
