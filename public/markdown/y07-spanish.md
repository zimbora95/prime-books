I N T R O D U C T I O N
Your first year of Spanish

This book assumes you know no Spanish at all. It does not assume you are slow. Those are different
things, and the difference is the whole design of the book.

Spanish is spoken by about 500 million people as a first language, and it is an official language in 20
countries. You already share a great deal with it: if you read Portuguese or English you will recognise
familia, restaurante, problema and diccionario the first time you see them. The work of Year 7 is not
to learn thousands of new words. It is to learn how Spanish puts words together, and to say them out
loud often enough that they stop being a code.

What one year gives you

By the end of this book you can introduce
yourself and someone else, describe your
family and what people look like, say
what time you do things, describe your
house and your town, ask for directions,
order food in a restaurant, talk about
what you do at the weekend, buy clothes,
talk about being online, say what the
weather is doing, and tell someone about
a school trip that has already happened.

That last one matters more than it looks.
Talking about the past is the step that turns a list of phrases into a language, and it arrives in Unit 5,
once you have enough words for it to be worth doing.

How the year is shaped

There are seven units. Unit 0 is a reference unit: the alphabet, the numbers, the days, the months and
the clock. You do not learn it in one lesson. You come back to it all year, which is why it is at the front.

Units 1 to 5 are the teaching units, and each one is built from topics numbered like 2.3. Every topic is a
small number of pages with one grammar point, a set of words, a reading, something to say out loud
and something to write. Units 1, 2 and 4 end with a revision block, because the school year has three
tests in it and revising is a skill you are taught here rather than left to invent. Unit 6 is three readings
for the holidays, written only with language you already have.

What you need

A pencil, an exercise book for longer writing, and a partner to talk to. Nothing else. Where a page asks
you to write on it, the space is real space, so use it.

You need three pieces of information about yourself: your name, your age and where you are from.
Each one has its own verb, and each verb is different from the English. That is the work of this topic.

ASÍ FUNCIONA
Here is how it works

QUESTION
ENGLISH
ANSWER PATTERN

¿Cómo te llamas?
What is your name?
Me llamo…

¿Cuántos años tienes?
How old are you?
Tengo… años.

¿De dónde eres?
Where are you from?
Soy de…

Learn each answer as one whole piece. Do not try to translate the parts yet: me llamo literally
means "I call myself", and knowing that does not help you say it faster.

Spanish leaves out the word for I. The verb ending already says who is speaking, so yo is only added for
emphasis or contrast.

Soy de Portugal. = I am from Portugal.
Tengo doce años. = I am twelve.

Add yo only to contrast: Yo soy de Lisboa, ella es de
Madrid.

The same goes for tú: ¿Y tú? means "And you?"

CUIDADO
The mistake to avoid

Age uses tener, to have. Country uses ser, to be. Swapping them is the commonest mistake of
the first term.

✓ Tengo once años.
✗ Soy once años.

✓ Soy de Portugal.
✗ Tengo de Portugal.

SUENA ASÍ
It sounds like this

llamo starts like the y in yes: "ya-mo". años has the ñ: "a-nyos". Without the tilde, anos means
something else entirely, so the tilde is not decoration.

The four structures

The four little verbs. English uses "is" and "has" for all of this. Spanish uses four different

STRUCTURE
THE QUESTION IT ANSWERS
EXAMPLE

es

Mi casa es moderna.

tiene
What does it have?

hay

En mi casa hay un jardín.

Here is how it works

Gustar works backwards. Spanish does not say "I like the park". It says "the park pleases me".
That sounds strange for about a week and then it is automatic, and it explains everything else on
this page.

ESPAÑOL
LITERALLY
ENGLISH

Me gusta el parque.
the park pleases me
I like the park.

Me gustan los parques.
the parks please me
I like parks.

Te gusta la plaza.
the square pleases you
You like the square.

Le gusta el centro.
the centre pleases him or her
He or she likes the centre.

So the verb agrees with the thing, not with the person. One thing: gusta. More than one thing:
gustan.

And an action is always singular. If what you like is a verb, it is always gusta, whatever follows.

Me gusta leer. → I like reading.
Me gusta jugar al fútbol. → I like playing football.

No me gusta estudiar por la noche.
Me gusta ir al parque los sábados.

CUIDADO
The mistake to avoid

¿Por qué? in two words with an accent asks the question. porque in one word with no accent
gives the answer. They sound almost the same and the difference is worth marks all the way to A
Level.

Comparar

More than, less than, the same as. Three shapes, and one irregular pair worth learning now.

El parque es más grande que la plaza.
Mi barrio es menos ruidoso que el centro.

La biblioteca es tan tranquila como mi dormitorio.
mejor = better, peor = worse. Never más bueno.

CONSEJOS
Advice for the paper

In the examination itself. Five things that gain marks, in the order you should do them.

Read the whole paper first. Two minutes, and it stops you writing the right answer in the
wrong place.

Do Parte D last but leave twenty five minutes for it. It is worth thirty marks and it is the part
that runs out of time.

In the reading, answer in Spanish, and short is fine. A copied phrase from the text that
answers the question scores.

Check every verb ending and every adjective agreement at the end. That check is worth more

CÓMO SE LEE
How to read

How to read a text you have not seen before. Four steps, in this order. They matter more than
any single word in the three texts that follow.

1. Read the whole thing once without stopping. Do not look anything up. You will understand
more than you expect.

2. Find the time markers first: ayer, normalmente, el año que viene. They tell you when each
part is happening.

3. For a word you do not know, look at the words on either side of it before you look at the
word itself. Most of the time the sentence has already told you.

4. Only then read the questions, and go back to find each answer. Never answer from memory:
the text is right there.

CUIDADO · POR QUÉ Y PORQUE
Why and because

¿Por qué? in two words, with an accent, asks the question. porque in one word, with no accent,
gives the answer. They sound almost the same and are written differently, and that difference is
worth ten marks a year.

¿Por qué estás contenta? Porque es mi cumpleaños.

DEL PATIO
From the courtyard

The opening question mark was made compulsory by the Real Academia Española in the second
edition of its Ortografía, in 1754, and it took almost a century to be used everywhere. Spanish
word order does not always signal that a question is coming, so a reader needs the warning at
the start of the sentence rather than at the end of it. It is a practical mark, not an ornament.
