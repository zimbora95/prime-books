Hello.

You are eight (or nearly nine). You already know your school street, your friends
and the games you play at break. Global Perspectives asks a bigger question: how
do people live and learn in other places, and what can we do that is fair and
kind?

meet children who get to school in different ways
practise talking and listening

The Learning Path · Look Outwards Club

Luggage tags: Learning, Journeys, Barriers, Helpers, Fairness.

◎IN THIS UNIT YOU WILL

1. Explain that education is more than books and tests.

You will notice learning at school, at home and in clubs, not only at a desk.

2. Compare ways children travel to school in different places.

Walk, bus, boat, bicycle: journeys are not the same for every child.

3. Name barriers that keep some children out of school.

Cost, distance, design and danger can block access; a barrier is not a personal
failure.

4. Choose a kind, realistic way to help.

Fair helpers listen first, then act with the people who already lead the work.

5. Plan a dream school that includes more learners.

You will design one page that removes a barrier and welcomes more children.

▮KEYWORDS

Five words for this unit. Say each one out loud, then find it again on the page
where it is taught.

education: growing knowledge and skills for life, not only school lessons

barrier: something that blocks access, such as cost, distance, danger or
building design

enquiry: careful asking and checking before you decide

inclusive: designed so that more people can take part

perspective: a way of seeing from somebody else's place and story

▸LET US FIND OUT

Education means growing knowledge and skills so you can live, work and care
for others. It happens in school, at home, in clubs and outdoors. Reading a
recipe, learning a song and practising a fair tackle are all education.

In a Lisbon classroom, children might measure flour for maths. On a farm
path, a child learns which plants need water. In a library in Coimbra, a
volunteer may help an adult learner practise Portuguese.

Some learning has a timetable and a teacher. Some happens because you
were curious and asked. Both count, and both take practice.

A barrier is something that blocks access. Barriers can include the cost of
books or uniforms, long distances, illness, work at home, conflict, and
buildings that are not safe for children with disabilities.

A barrier is not a personal failure. It is a problem a community can work on.

Fees: when families cannot pay, children may stay at home.

the school morning.

## TIME CAPSULE

**HISTORY**

Long ago, most schools had one room, one teacher and
one fire. Pupils sat on wooden benches and wrote on small
slate boards with chalk. There were no computers, no lights
and no school buses.

Some children walked many kilometres to school, and some
never went at all. In many places girls were kept at home to
work, and poor families could not spare their children from
the fields.

·
Which of these things have changed by 2026?

Around the world, children travel to school in many ways. In
Bangladesh some pupils row a boat along flooded roads. In
Nepal they cross high rope bridges over rivers. In Kenya
many walk several kilometres before the first lesson.

Journeys can be long, risky or costly, and that is a barrier to
education. The United Nations says every child has the right
to reach a classroom safely.

Boat, bridge, footpath,

same goal.

Unit 2 · Improving communication

The Talking Circle · Look Outwards Club

Luggage tags: Talk, Listen, Body, Questions, Turns, Styles.

◎IN THIS UNIT YOU WILL

1. Name talking and listening as equal skills.

You will practise say-it-back so a partner feels heard, not just waited for.

2. Spot body language and tone that change a message.

Face, hands and voice can match or fight the words you choose.

3. Use calm phrases to cool small conflicts.

Tools such as "I feel… when…" help without name-calling.

4. Ask open questions like a careful detective.

Open questions need reasons, not only yes or no.

5. Take turns and include quieter voices.

Roles and timers make group talk fairer for everyone.

6. Respect different communication styles.

Some people talk, draw or think first; different is not wrong.

▮KEYWORDS

Five words for this unit. Say each one out loud, then find it again on the page
where it is taught.

body language: messages your face, hands and posture send without words

tone: how a voice sounds: kind, sharp, tired, teasing

open question: a question that needs more than yes or no

closed question: a question that can be answered yes or no

Talking and listening

↺DO YOU REMEMBER?

Which is harder for you on most days: speaking or listening?

◉BOOTS ON!

Turn to a partner. They talk for thirty seconds about breakfast. You may only

▸LET US FIND OUT

A conversation needs two engines: clear speech and full attention. Listening
means eyes, ears and patience. Interrupting is like snatching a ball mid-pass.

You know you were listening if you can say the other person's idea back, in
your own words, and they say yes, that is what I meant. Teachers sometimes
use talk partners because explaining an idea out loud strengthens memory.

**UNIT 4 - THE WORLD OF WORK**

## TIME CAPSULE

**HISTORY**

Long ago, most people did the same work as their parents, in the place
where they were born. A baker's child learned to bake; a farmer's child
learned to farm. Tools were simple: ovens, anvils, needles and ploughs.
Work filled the whole family's day, from sunrise to sunset.

Then machines changed everything. Trains carried food to far cities,
factories made goods faster than any pair of hands, and new jobs were
born: engine drivers, telegraph operators, shopkeepers. Many old trades
are still with us today, just done with new tools.

Baker, blacksmith and farmer:
work before machines.

Around the world, people do different jobs because places are different.

Coffee grows on mountains in Ethiopia and Colombia, and is drunk in
