WELCOME
Come out to the hedge bank

The Hollow, early on a bright morning, before anyone has arrived.

There is a bank at the edge of a field. Grass grows tall on it. Bramble
and clover grow there too. Under the grass there are stones, and dry
leaves, and crumbly brown earth.

All sorts of things live on that bank. Some of them are very small.
Some of them are hard to see at all, until you look closely.

This book is about looking closely.

Six animals live on the hedge bank, and all six of them are curious.
They want to know what things are made of, and why the Sun feels
warm, and what makes a sound, and which things float. They ask
questions, and then they find out.

You are going to do the same.

WELCOME

WHO YOU WILL MEET
Six curious neighbours

Bram, Nib, Pod, Tor, Wren and Dart.

**Bram the badger. He digs, so he knows what is under the grass. The brass**
hand lens is his.

**Nib the wood mouse. His ears are enormous. He hears things nobody else**
notices.

**Pod the snail. Pod is slow, and touches everything, so Pod knows how things**
feel.

**Tor the toad. Tor sits very still on a warm stone and watches the sky all day.**

**Wren. She is the smallest, and the quickest, and she sings the loudest of**
them all.

**Dart the frog. Dart pushes, pulls and jumps, and never stays still for long.**

You will see all six of them again and again as you work through this book. Each one helps with the
science they know best.

1.3  Plants and light

⚲Let us find out

A plant cannot walk about to find its food. So it makes its own,
## and it makes it out of light.

The leaves do that job. A leaf is flat and wide so it can catch as
much light as it can.

Soil is important too, but not as food. Soil holds the plant up, and
holds the water the roots drink. A plant does not eat soil.

A plant kept in the dark grows tall, thin and pale, and then it dies.
It is reaching for light it cannot find.

2.1 SOUNDS ALL AROUND US

2.1  Sounds all around us

WAKE UP

YOUR
SENSES

Hum with your hand on your throat. Can you feel the
shaking under your fingers?

⚲Let us find out

Sounds are all around you. A sound is made when something
## shakes. The shaking is very fast, and it is often too small to see.

Pluck a tight string and you can watch it shake. Tap a drum and
the skin shakes. Put one finger on the string and the shaking
stops, so the sound stops too.

## You hear sounds with your ears. You have two ears, and they help

FOR TEACHERS
About this book

**Science & Lab, Year 1 supports the Cambridge Primary Science curriculum framework at Stage 1,**
across the Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Earth and Space strands, with Thinking and Working
Scientifically running through every topic rather than sitting apart from them.

**How the book is built. Six units, twenty-four topics. Each topic runs to three pages: one teaching**
page, then two pages of practice. Every topic carries at least twelve practice items, one modelled
enquiry with a stated finding, and at least one How do you know? prompt. Every unit ends with a word
list, a review, and a page for pupils to record what they can now do.

**What the enquiry steps are for. The five steps on page 11 recur in every unit and again on the closing**
pages. They are deliberately the same five words each time, so that by the summer term a pupil can
name the step they are on without prompting.

**Safety. Practical work is written for five and six year olds under direct supervision. Carry out your**
own risk assessment first, and follow your school policy. The Sun rule in Unit 4 is the one to repeat
aloud at every outdoor session rather than reading once.

**Misconceptions this book tackles head on. That plants eat soil (Unit 1, revisited in Unit 4). That**
sound travels through nothing (Unit 2). That heavy things always sink (Unit 6). That magnets pull
every metal (Unit 6, where a copper coin and aluminium foil are in the kit for that purpose). In each
case the wrong idea is named, put in a character's mouth, and then tested, because a misconception
that is never spoken aloud is never given up.
