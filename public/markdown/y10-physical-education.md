## 1.1 Introduction

Futsal grew in Uruguay and Brazil as indoor football on a hard court. FIFA governs the international game. The
low-bounce size 4 ball and the kick-in restart keep the ball on the floor and force a first touch that is close to the
foot.

A school match is five including a goalkeeper. There is no offside. Unlimited rolling substitutions keep the
intensity high. Year 10 futsal is not outdoor football played inside. It is its own sport.

Safety is footwear, no jewellery, and no slide tackles. The court does not give way. You do.

Futsal: typical school court about 28–40 m × 16–20 m

**KEY POINTS**

Five players, rolling substitutes, no offside.

Kick-in, not throw-in. Four seconds in the full game.

Low-bounce ball: kill the bounce, then look.

Hard-court safety: no slides, clean indoor shoes.

## 1.2 Warm-ups

A futsal warm-up raises pulse and rehearses the shapes of the lesson: short passing, stopping under control, and
a change of direction.

Start with easy jogging and dynamic mobility for ankles, hips and calves. Add the ball while you can still talk. A
full-speed shot as the first touch of the lesson is too late and too sharp.

A futsal warm-up looks like the game: short steps, a ball, and a change of direction.

**KEY POINTS**

Pulse raiser: 3 to 4 minutes.

Dynamic mobility, not long static holds.

Ball activation on the move.

One short reaction game before the main drill.

## 1.3 Ball control

First touch is a pass to yourself. Cushion with the inside of the foot, or the sole, and send the ball into the space
you already chose. A heavy first touch turns you into a defender.

Dribbling is done in a tight box. Sole to stop and roll, inside and outside to change direction. Keep the ball within
a step. Head up between touches.

First touch with the whole body in the picture: legs, balance, and a head that is already up.

**KEY POINTS**

Cushion, do not stab.

First touch away from the arriving press.

Sole, inside, outside: three tools, one close ball.

Head up between touches.

## 1.4 Passing

Passing is short, early and often. The inside-foot pass is the base. Add the sole pass when the ball is under you
and a driven floor pass when you must beat a line of pressure.

The receiver is part of the pass. Show a target, call, and move again. Standing still after a pass is how attacks die
on a small court.

Passing lanes appear and disappear quickly. The player without the ball creates the next one.

**KEY POINTS**

Inside foot for accuracy, ankle locked.

Pass to the safe foot, away from the marker.

Move after you pass: give an angle.

Use both feet.

## 1.5 Shooting and finishing

Finishing is often from a crowd. Accuracy beats wild power. The laces drive is for a clear sight of goal. The inside-
foot placement is for the far corner when the goalkeeper has stepped out.

Standing foot beside the ball, head still, follow through toward the target. Practise both feet. Follow the shot:
rebounds are part of the finish.

A composed finish starts with the standing foot and a still head, not with a longer backswing.

**KEY POINTS**

Pick a target before you swing.

Low shots are harder for the goalkeeper on a hard court.

Follow in.

Do not shoot when a simple pass creates a better chance.

## 1.6 Goalkeeping

The futsal goalkeeper is a field player with hands in a small area. Stance is low, hands ready, feet ready to close
the angle. Distribution starts the next attack: a calm roll beats a hopeful punt.

Every outfield player should spend time in goal. Understanding the goalkeeper's picture makes you a more
honest attacker.

The goal is small, so positioning beats spectacular diving.

**KEY POINTS**

Ready stance: knees bent, weight forward.

Get big. Do not dive early.

Distribute with the hands when you can.

Communicate: left, right, time, keeper.

## 1.7 Attacking strategies

Attacks need width, depth and a spare player. One holds, one supports underneath, one stretches the far side.
The fourth reads whether to join or to balance.

A Year 10 pattern is pass and move behind the first defender. Do not all run toward the ball. The court is already
small.

Attacks breathe when players occupy different lines, not the same square metre.

**KEY POINTS**

Create a diamond, not a crowd.

Use the pivot as a back-to-goal link.

Switch play when one side is blocked.

Shoot when the extra player is free.

## 1.8 Defensive strategies

Defence starts on turnover. The nearest player presses. The next covers the passing line. The rest drop into a
compact shape between ball and goal.

If your player receives facing your goal, you arrived too late. Show them onto the weaker foot and do not dive in.

A compact defensive shape leaves no easy split pass through the middle.

**KEY POINTS**

Press, cover, balance.

Show the attacker onto the weaker foot.

Do not dive in.

Talk: I have the ball, you have the next pass.

## 1.9 Game scenarios

Match play is where skills become decisions. Play 3 v 3 and 4 v 4 before 5 v 5. Give each game one constraint:
two-touch, weaker-foot finish, or a pass before a shot.

Rotate roles. The quiet player on the wing this game is the pivot next game.

A constrained small-sided game teaches one decision at a time.

**KEY POINTS**

Small-sided first, then 5 v 5.

One constraint per game.

Everyone plays. Everyone keeps.

Stop the game to coach one picture, then play on.

## Word list

These words are used in the units. The definition matches the way the book uses the word.

**Aerobic system**
Energy system that uses oxygen; dominates longer, steadier work.

**ATP-PC**
Immediate energy system for efforts of about 0 to 10 seconds.

**Down**
One play in flag football. A short set of downs is given to advance or score.

**FITT**
Frequency, intensity, time, type: a way to plan training.

**Flag football**
Non-contact American football in which a pulled flag ends the play.

**Korf**
The basket in korfball. It has no backboard.

**Lactic system**
Energy system for hard work of about 10 seconds to 2 minutes.

**Lever**
A bone-and-joint system that applies force. Used to explain throws and take-offs.

**Negative split**
Running the second half of a race at least as quickly as the first.

**Pivot**
Futsal attacker who plays with their back to goal and links play.

**Rebound**
Winning the ball after a missed shot at a korf or ring.

**Route**
A named path run by a receiver in flag football.

**Sector**
The legal landing area for a throw in athletics.

**SPORT**
Specificity, progression, overload, reversibility, tedium: training principles.

**Travelling**
Moving illegally with the ball in korfball (there is no dribble).
