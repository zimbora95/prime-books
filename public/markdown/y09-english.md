UNIT 1 · SESSION 1.1

Reading fiction: genre and setting

Learn

A genre is a type of story with recognisable features. Readers use those features to form expectations within a

sentence or two, and skilled writers play with those expectations deliberately.

✦
G E T T I N G STA RT E D

A story opens with someone going down a staircase they have been warned about. In pairs, discuss:

what genre do you expect, and what made you expect it?

what has the writer already made you feel, with almost no information?

what is the single most powerful thing a writer can withhold?

❝
E X T R A CT · ' T H E C I ST E R N ' (O P E N I N G )

They reached the cistern faster than Ines had expected, which she did not like. Nothing

about this had been slow so far, and slow was how she preferred her mistakes.

"Down there," said Bruno, and the torchlight went with his arm. "The Cartographer says you

are the one who can read it."

It flashed through her mind that she had never told him her name. She had been careful
about that, careful for eleven months, and now here she was at the mouth of a hole in the

ground with a man who knew what she could do.

"Be back here at nine tomorrow night," he said. And with that, he was gone.

She knew she would be there.

Which genre of fiction do you think 'The Cistern' belongs to? Discuss in
pairs, considering:

the setting

UNIT 1 · SESSION 1.2

Story structure and flashback

Learn

Structure is the order in which a writer reveals things, and it shapes the reader's experience as powerfully as the

events themselves. A writer chooses when to introduce a character, how to handle time, and what to hold back.

A flashback is a section that goes back in time to explain something. Used well, it answers one question while raising

another.

Writers give explicit information: details stated directly. In the first

she has an ability someone wants. Write a paragraph explaining:
