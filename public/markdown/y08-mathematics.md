UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.1

Factors, multiples and primes

Learn

A factor of a number divides it exactly. A multiple of a number is what you get by multiplying it by 1, 2, 3 and so on.
Some numbers have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves. These are the prime numbers, and they are the atoms of

arithmetic. The primes up to 30 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and 29.

Every whole number greater than 1 can be written as a product of prime numbers in exactly one way. This is called
writing it as a product of prime factors.

✦

S C E N A R I O

Every year, the organisers of the Sardinha Festival in a small Portuguese town have to arrange the sardine
grills in perfect rows. If they have 84 grills, they can make 7 rows of 12, or 12 rows of 7, or 6 rows of 14. The
numbers that divide 84 exactly decide which arrangements work. This unit is about the hidden structure of

numbers like 84: their factors, their multiples, and the prime building blocks underneath them all.

∎
W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 .1

Write 84 as a product of prime factors.

84 ÷ 2 = 42
42 ÷ 2 = 21
21 ÷ 3 = 7
7 ÷ 7 = 1
so 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 2² × 3 × 7

Why: Dividing out each prime in turn breaks 84 down to its building blocks. The result is unique: no other
collection of primes multiplies to 84.

W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 . 2

Work out (−3) × (−8) + (−48) ÷ 6.

(−3) × (−8) = 24      same signs: positive
(−48) ÷ 6 = −8        different signs: negative
24 + (−8) = 16

Why: Each multiplication and division is done first, following the sign rules; the addition comes last.

UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.3

Square roots and cube roots

Learn

A square root undoes squaring: √169 = 13 because 13² = 169. A cube root undoes cubing: ∛343 = 7 because 7³ = 343.
Most numbers are not perfect powers, but you can trap their roots between two whole numbers.

W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 . 3

Between which two consecutive whole numbers does √200 lie?

14² = 196 and 15² = 225
196 < 200 < 225,  so 14 < √200 < 15

Why: Because 200 sits between two consecutive squares, its root sits between the corresponding roots.

UNIT 1 · TOPIC 1.4

Indices

Learn

An index (plural indices) tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself: 3⁴ means 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. The 4 is

the index, and 3 is the base. Indices have three beautiful laws that make hard calculations easy.

To multiply powers of the same base, add the indices: 3⁴ × 3² = 3⁶.

To divide powers of the same base, subtract the indices: 5⁷ ÷ 5³ = 5⁴.

Any number to the power zero is 1: 7⁰ = 1.

∎
W O R K E D E X A M P L E 1 . 4

Work out: a. 2⁴ × 2³ b. (3²)³ c. 10⁵ ÷ 10²

a. 2⁴ × 2³ = 2⁷ = 128
b. (3²)³ = 3⁶ = 729
c. 10⁵ ÷ 10² = 10³ = 1000

Why: Adding, multiplying and subtracting indices turns long multiplications into one-step index operations.

**●**

T H I N K L I K E A M AT H E M AT I C I A N

The number 64 is special. It is 8², it is 4³, and it is 2⁶. So 64 is a perfect square, a perfect cube and a perfect
sixth power, all at once. Can you find another number less than 1000 that is both a perfect square and a
perfect cube? Investigate and explain your method.

GLOSSARY

Unit 1

The mathematical words this unit has taught you, gathered in one place.

common factor: a number that is a factor of two or more numbers.

common multiple: a number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

consecutive: following one after another in order.

cube number: a number made by multiplying an integer by itself twice, such as 27 = 3³.

cube root: the number that cubes to give the value under the root, as in ∛27 = 3.

digit: any of the symbols 0 to 9 used to write numbers.

divisible: able to be divided exactly, with no remainder.

factor: a number that divides another exactly.

highest common factor (HCF): the largest common factor of two or more numbers.

index: the small raised number showing a power, as in 5².

integer: a positive or negative whole number, or zero.

inverse: the number that undoes another; the inverse of 4 is −4.

inverse operation: an operation that reverses another; subtraction is the inverse operation of addition.

lowest common multiple (LCM): the smallest common multiple of two or more numbers.

multiple: the result of multiplying a number by a positive integer.

number line: a line on which numbers are placed in order.

product: the result of a multiplication.

quotient: the result of a division.

square number: a number made by multiplying an integer by itself, such as 36 = 6².

square root: the number that squares to give the value under the root, as in √36 = 6.

test for divisibility: a quick rule that tells you whether one number divides another without doing the division.
