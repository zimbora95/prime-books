Standard A for Years 1 to 4

One locked specification for every Prime Books primary title, Years 1 to 4, set for how we actually print: BookVault, perfect bound paperback.

This page is the short, working version. Everything a teacher, a designer or a build tool needs to know is either on this page or one click away. The old publishing standard page stays as the full house record for Years 1 to 12.

In one paragraph. Every Prime Books primary title is the same book underneath: the same size to the millimetre, the same two fonts, the same four or more units each wearing its own fixed colour, the same page order from cover to back cover, and the same kind of page for the same job in every subject. What changes from book to book is the teaching, not the furniture. Year 1 to Year 4 are four steps on one ladder: the text gets a little smaller, the writing lines get tighter, the pictures carry more information, and the pupils are asked to do more. Nothing else changes.

Inside a unit, always in this order: opener, get started, concept and model, your turn, extension, applied project, reflection, word bank. A subject with no need for the extension page simply does not have one. It never invents a different shape.

The safe area, and the header we are deleting

This is why the unit opener needed changing. On the Unit 1 opening page of Physical Education Year 1 (Moving well, physical page 12) the tracked eyebrow starts at x 36.8 pt, which is 13 mm from the trim edge and 7 mm inside the gutter. In a perfect-bound book that lettering disappears into the spine. It is also saying something the page already says directly underneath it, in the big unit title.

The rule now. The unit name appears once at the top of a unit page. On the opener: the unit eyebrow, the numeral, the unit title and the subtitle. On a topic page: the running head in the 47 pt band carries Unit 1, Moving Well and the kicker carries UNIT 1, TOPIC 1.1 with the topic title, and the unit name is not printed a third time in the body. Everything sits inside the safe area, so nothing is lost in the spine, and the space we recover goes to the teaching.

And the opposite guard rail: no blank pages. If a book has to reach a legal page count, the extra page is filled with something usable, a workspace, an extra glossary spread or a source list, never with white space. A page that exists is a page that teaches.

The contents page is the book's business card

Page 3 carries a colour bar with one segment per unit, then one tinted card per unit giving the unit number, its starting page, its name, a one-line summary of the strand and its topic rows. Front matter and back matter sit underneath as plain dot-leader rows. Nothing on that page is typed by hand: the page numbers come out of the finished PDF, so the contents can never point at the wrong page again.

One honest warning. The print rules and the old house rule disagree in two places, and the print rules now win. BookVault wants a page count one less than a multiple of twelve, which is always an odd number, while the old gate fails any odd page count. And the old gate checks a 612 × 792 pt page, while the printer's trim is 216 × 279 mm. Both gate rules have to change before a BookVault-compliant book can pass, and that is the first job on the list.

PART SIXThe cast, the codes and the references

One cast, that subject's cast

The welcome page introduces four to six named characters with a portrait and one line each. They are chosen for the subject, not shared across the year group: a Science cast, a Music cast, a Physical Education cast. Once a character is approved, that exact character is reused in every scene it appears in, and consistency is held by an image reference, not by memory.

How the image reference works. The approved character sheet is passed to google/gemini-3-pro-image through OpenRouter's chat endpoint as an image alongside the scene prompt, and the returned art holds the same species, clothing and colouring. I tested it: the same squirrel in the same mustard knit jumper came back in a new scene. The plain image generation model, openai/gpt-image-2.5-sunburst, silently ignores a reference image, so it is used for new plates only, always at quality medium, which renders lettering correctly and keeps the cost per page sane.

QR codes, at the point of the activity

Codes are not collected on one page at the back. A code sits inside the topic page, next to the instruction it belongs to, roughly one per unit, more in practical subjects, and none where there is nothing worth showing. Each one is captioned with what it shows and how long it runs, in the child's reading size, at least 74 pt square on a white card. Every address is fetched and every code is decoded back out of the rendered page before the book goes to print, and every target is also printed in full in the back matter, so the book still works if a link dies.

References, straight after the glossary

A references section follows the illustrated glossary and lists every information source the book used: books, websites, datasets, and the credit and licence for every image that is not ours. One source per row, laid out like the contents rows, each marked with the page or topic it supports. It is the printed proof of where the facts came from, and it is what makes the QR list safe to print.

PART SEVENPictures

Narrative scenes, objects and characters are original ink and watercolour in the Beatrix Potter direction: fine contours, transparent washes, warm muted pigments, cream paper, woodland animals in believable clothes, the same cast and clothing all the way through a book. The picture does a teaching job: if a page shows how to hold a brush, the picture shows the exact safe grip, tool and angle.

Two things stay true whatever the style is. Photographs of real artworks, places and specimens stay as photographs when they teach something an invented picture cannot. Diagrams, maths models, letterforms and colour wheels stay precise and their labels stay as live text. Never stretch a picture, never reuse one hero scene as filler, never let the model put lettering inside the artwork. Years 1 and 2 get large actions and generous space. Years 3 and 4 get more process, comparison and labelled evidence, in the same medium.

PART EIGHTWhat is locked, and what is still open

Locked now, and not up for renegotiation per book

  • Trim 216 × 279 mm, media 222 × 285 mm, 3 mm bleed
  • Mirrored margins with a 20 mm gutter
  • Page count in the 11, 23, 35, 47 series
  • Fredoka for display, Andika for reading, Poppins for covers only
  • The page map in Part Two, in that order
  • One colour per unit, rung number fixed across the series
  • Contents on page 3 with a bar and one card per unit
  • Every page inside the safe area: 20 mm gutter, 5 mm elsewhere
  • The unit name printed once at the top of a unit page
  • No blank pages, ever: padding is always usable content
  • One cast per subject, on the welcome page, reused through the book by image reference
  • QR codes at the point of the activity, captioned, verified, and printed in full at the back
  • A references section after the glossary, one source per row
  • Folio badge: 24 pt circle centred at (550, 758)
  • The rules that override everything: never delete or move teacher-placed content, never shrink text to fit, readability before aesthetics

Decided today, on your delegation

  • Palette rungs 5 and 6: sage and ochre, as shown above. The ochre's mid tone was darkened to the house ochre #9A6A12 so a white folio numeral keeps a readable 4.7 to 1 contrast.
  • No size migration. The authoring page stays US Letter 612 × 792 pt. The printer's 216 × 279 mm trim is the same physical book to within 0.4 mm, so the pack builder normalises it and nothing in the corpus gets rebuilt for a rounding.
  • Extent is a formula, not a fixed budget. Front matter 7 pages, plus the units at 8 to 10 pages each in Year 1 rising to 12 to 14 in Year 4, plus 12 to 14 back matter pages, then round up to the next legal count. Never round down, never pad with a blank page.
  • The spine is a measured number. Use BookVault's calculator for the stock ordered, then check the first physical proof with calipers and correct the figure if it is out by more than half a millimetre. A wrong spine fails the whole print job.
  • The year ladder is locked as authoring guidance and re-measured as soon as the first Year 1 book is rebuilt, so it stops being a proposal and becomes a measurement.

One item still open

  • The real spine width for the paper you actually order. That number is not mine to guess and it changes with the stock, so it is the single figure to confirm before the first upload.

Before a book is called done

  • Every teacher-placed sentence, image and activity is still there, on its own page.
  • The contents, the index and every cross-reference point at the real page, taken from the built PDF.
  • The page count lands in the print series, and the last interior page is clear for the production barcode.
  • Every image is either credited or recorded as our own, at 300 dpi or better at the size it is placed.
  • Every QR code is decoded back out of the printed page, and every URL opens.
  • Black text is K only, transparency is flattened, fonts are embedded, no crop marks.
  • One physical proof copy has been held in the hand before the rest of the year is built.
