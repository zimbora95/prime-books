One standard, Years 1 to 4

Four years, one collection. This page is the working specification: what a Prime Books title is, what never changes between subjects, what is freed to be that subject's own, and the exact numbers a builder must hit. Everything here is locked except one open item.

Locked 14 September 2026 Edition A-2.3 Identity layer added Print route BookVault, perfect bound 1 item still open

In one breath. Every primary title is the same book underneath: the same size to the millimetre, the same three fonts, one fixed colour per unit number, the same page order from cover to back cover, and the same kind of page for the same job in every subject. What changes is the teaching — and the identity: each title gets its own voice, its own cast, its own signature page.

What the books look like

A Prime Book is recognised across a room, before the title is legible. That comes from four decisions that never vary: the frame, the year band, the unit colours, and the furniture.

The cover frame, and the one thing that changes

The frame is identical on every title: a thin gold double fillet, the picture held inside it, the publisher matter on the back. The year is announced by a single band of colour, the same in every subject. The picture is new for each subject, and it is the only part of the cover that is.

Front and back covers are also pages of the book PDF, so they are stripped out of the interior file before it goes to the printer, and the interior is counted without them.

The furniture

Type ladder, folio badge, unit band, safe area. A teacher learns it once and it holds for four years.

Always new
The picture

Commissioned per subject in the house illustration direction. It is the book's face, not its identity.

The illustration direction

Original Beatrix Potter direction: fine ink contours, transparent watercolour, warm muted pigments, lightly textured cream paper. No text of any kind inside generated art. Grade 1 to 2 gets large actions and short sequences; grade 3 to 4 gets process detail, comparisons and labelled evidence. Authentic photographs and precise technical diagrams are kept as they are, never redrawn as paintings.

All six rungs
Gold stays gold

The gold accent never changes with the unit. Six different colours still read as one publisher because the gold, the type and the furniture are constant.

The one thing not to confuse: the cover's year stripe is its own system — amber, sky, orange, green — and is never used as a unit colour inside the book.

Contrast, not taste. Rungs 5 and 6 were locked with the white folio numeral in mind: sage measures 4.85 to 1 and ochre 4.7 to 1, so a white numeral stays readable on both. When a rung is proposed, it is proposed with its contrast figure.

Years 1 to 4 are one book at four ages. The reading text shrinks by one and a half points across four years, the writing lines halve in height, and the page grows from one sentence with a big picture into a short, structured paragraph with evidence.

Inside a unit, page by page

This is where the standard earns its keep. A unit has a fixed order and a varied rhythm, and both are visible from across the room.

The contents page

  • Page 3, always. One colour bar segment per unit, equal width.
  • One card per unit: UNIT n, its start page, its name, a one-line strand summary, its topic rows, and Unit n, How did it go?
  • Front and back matter as plain dot-leader rows, glossary and references included.
  • Every page number read out of the built PDF and verified against the real index. Nothing typed by hand.

The unit name appears once

On the opener: the unit eyebrow, the numeral, the unit title and the subtitle. On a topic page: the running head in the 47 pt band carries Unit n, Unit name and the kicker carries UNIT n, TOPIC x.y with the topic title — and the unit name is not printed a third time in the body. This is a measured correction, not a preference: on the Year 1 Physical Education opener the tracked eyebrow started at x 36.8 pt, which is 13 mm from the trim and 7 mm inside BookVault's 20 mm gutter — lettering that disappears into the spine, repeating what the big title already says.

The safe area

No blank pages, ever

If a book has to reach a legal page count, the extra pages are filled with something usable: workspace, an extra glossary spread, a source list. A page that exists is a page that teaches. The very last interior page is the exception — it is kept clear in its lower area because BookVault prints its production barcode there.

The rhythm: no worksheet factory

A standard that repeats one exercise layout every ten pages is a worksheet factory, not a book. The rhythm is written as a weight string and checked before the book is built.

E — energy: opener, hook, big question D — dense: model, worked example, concept R — response: the child works, writes, draws, moves B — breath: one large image, few words S — signature: this subject's own page
  • No page type twice in consecutive positions; none more than twice inside one unit.
  • A unit of 8 to 14 pages carries at least four distinct page types.
  • Exactly one signature page and at least one breath page per unit. A breath page is one large image and no more than 25 words.
  • Never two dense teaching pages in a row, never three response pages in a row.
  • An opener must earn the turn: a question, an object or a promise — never a title alone.
  • Across a whole book, no layout runs unchanged on more than two units.

A four-page unit reads E D R S, and the breath page rotates position between units.

QR codes

  • At the point of the activity, beside the instruction it belongs to — not collected at the back.
  • Roughly one per unit of 8 to 14 pages, more in practical subjects, none where there is nothing worth showing.
  • Minimum 74 × 74 pt on a white card, 4 pt inset, quiet zone respected, captioned with what it shows and how long it runs.
  • Every URL fetched and every code decoded back out of the rendered page before publishing — and every target printed as a readable address in the back matter, so the book survives a dead link.

The identity layer

Edition A-2.3 adds the rule that stops the standard becoming a template: consistency, not uniformity. Two lists, and nothing crosses between them. No two Prime Books titles may read as the same book with a different subject on the cover.

Fixed in every book
The architecture

Trim, media, bleed and the safe area. The font set and the type ladder. Folio geometry and the unit band. The page map and the order of front and back matter. The contents component, the references section and the QR rules. Unit colour rung n = unit n, across the series. The cover frame. And the two rules that override everything: never delete or move teacher-placed content, never shrink text to fit.

Declared per book
The identity

Voice — the narrator's temperament and the phrasing of every instruction and caption. Cast and place — four to six named characters of that subject, plus one recurring place. Signature page — one page type unique to that subject, once per unit. Rhythm — the page-turn tempo of each unit. Material — the objects, textures and two accent tints the subject's art is built from. Cover picture — what the frame holds for this subject.

The identity block is declared in the book's own build file before a single page is built, and is recorded per title — never copied from another subject. One integer per book seeds QR placement, breath pages and the surprise element: varied between books, reproducible in the file, never random at render time.

One cast, that subject's cast

Consistency is held by an image reference, not by memory. A character that must reappear is generated with the approved character sheet attached as a reference image. There is exactly one route on the system that actually holds a character, because the house scene model ignores a reference image entirely: scenes stay on the house model at medium quality, and the reference route is used only when an existing character has to appear again.

What separates an award-winning book from a competent one

Every unit
Five things, on purpose

One curiosity hook a child wants answered. One moment of genuine child agency, not a comprehension gap-fill. One real audience or purpose beyond the mark scheme. One image a child will remember. One question the child asks back.

Every book
Six things, on purpose

A declared identity: voice, cast and place, signature page. A rhythm that varies. Authentic evidence kept authentic: photographs, specimens, real artworks, real data. Visible progress the child can see. Teacher rescue notes: the one thing to say when a class is stuck. Credits and references that survive printing.

The test. Say out loud what a child will remember from this book. If the answer is a worksheet, the book is not finished.

Build a book

This is the order of work, the brief a builder is given, and the test a finished book has to pass before it is called done.

The order of work

  • Declare the identity block for the title, in the book's own build file.
  • Lock the unit list and the page budget per unit; work out the extent and round up to a legal count.
  • Build the pages on the standard: trim, safe area, type ladder, unit rung, page map, unit order.
  • Keep every teacher-placed sentence, image and activity exactly where it was placed.
  • Generate nothing that contains text; keep real photographs and technical diagrams authentic.
  • Decode every QR code back out of the built page, and fetch every URL.
  • Run the checker, fix what it names, then look at the rendered pages with your own eyes.
The builder's brief

Before a book is called done

  • Every teacher-placed sentence, image and activity is still there, on its own page.
  • The contents, the index and every cross-reference point at the real page, taken from the built PDF.
  • The page count lands in the print series, and the last interior page is clear for the production barcode.
  • Every image is credited or recorded as our own, at 300 dpi or better at the size it is placed.
  • Every QR code is decoded back out of the printed page, and every URL opens.
  • Black text is K only, transparency is flattened, fonts are embedded, no crop marks.
  • The standard is visible on the page, not only in the checker's report.
  • One physical proof copy has been held in the hand before the rest of the year is built.

Two columns, and they mean different things

On the point of situation
Checks

The machine verdict. The checker measures the published PDF against the locked tokens and the bot runs it across the library every night. Clean means it passed every hard check: page size, embedded fonts, folio, a contents bar matching the book's own unit count, a unit-colour opener, every word inside the safe area, a cover without a folio and a labelled back cover. A number means that many hard failures. It covers Years 1 to 4 only — Years 5 to 13 are a different standard and show no verdict rather than a misleading one.

On the point of situation
Standardised

The teacher's sign-off, and only the teacher's. Clicking the column records it server-side. The bot never writes that file, because it cannot see whether teacher content survived, whether an image is licensed, whether a colour reads on paper, whether a QR code scans off paper, or whether a proof was held. Clean means ready for sign-off. Clean is not certified.

One item still open. The real spine width for the paper actually ordered. It is not a number to guess and it changes with the stock, so it is the single figure to confirm before the first upload.
